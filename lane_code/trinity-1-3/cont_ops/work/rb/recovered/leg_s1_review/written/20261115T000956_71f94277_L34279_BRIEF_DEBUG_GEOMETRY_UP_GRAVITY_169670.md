# Debugging: why the routed read's geometry refuses an ARKitScenes capture (Orchard pilot 169670)

## Background

A spatial vision-language model ("the leg", step-544 export) answers some question types by calling its own heads and perception tools; a "routed reader" serves those calls from a frozen geometry tower (G3T pose and depth) conditioned by the checkpoint's pose, depth, scale and gravity heads. Before any item is read, the reader builds one geometry profile per capture. Pilot 7 restored the model, passed its parity gate, built geometry for four captures (three ScanNet "d1", one ScanNet++ "d2") in about one second each, and then stopped on the first ARKitScenes ("d3") capture, `d3/41069053`, with `GeometryError: reference_camera_up_opposes_gravity`. That check is in the sealed trainer tree: `/scratch/jjyeung/orch_cd/leg_s1_tree/work/tree_s1_admissionfix_168872/student_pilot/geometry_profiles.py` line 96: with `R` the rotation part of `camera_to_world` for the 32 frames, it raises when `-R[0, 2, 1] <= 0`, that is, when the reference camera's image-up direction has no upward component in the gravity-aligned world. No earlier pilot included an ARKitScenes capture. 47 of the read's 150 distance items and part of its camera items sit on ARKitScenes captures, and ARKitScenes is about half of the official benchmark, so I need to know what this refusal means before changing anything.

Known fact from an earlier project record: the ARKitScenes videos in our training source are 480x640 portrait re-encodes of a 256x192 landscape stream; the adapter that joins them to ground-truth poses declares a 90-degree rotation and a 2.5x resize.

## Questions, each with file and line or with numbers from the returned record

A. **What happened numerically.** From the returned raw geometry of `d3/41069053` (the fifth raw geometry NPZ is in the failed return) and whatever the return records of the profile: the reference camera's rotation, the value of `-R[0, 2, 1]`, the roll of the reference camera about its optical axis relative to gravity, and the gravity estimate used. Is the camera rolled by about 90 degrees (a sideways-held or rotated image), upside down, or is the gravity estimate itself wrong? Compare with the four captures that passed.

B. **Where the orientation comes from.** Trace how `camera_to_world` and the gravity alignment are built for a capture: the raw tower cache (how it was produced for ARKitScenes captures and with which frame orientation), the reader's `geometry.py`, and the checkpoint's gravity head. Then trace what the frames look like: are the ARKitScenes frames the reader feeds the model and the tower upright, or rotated by 90 degrees? Compare with what the leg's training rows used for ARKitScenes scenes (frame orientation, pose convention, any declared rotation in the pose-stream or routing rows' metadata). Establish which holds: (a) the capture's frames are stored rotated and nothing in the read path applies the declared rotation, so the tower sees a sideways scene (a reader or data-preparation mismatch against training); (b) training had the same orientation and the sealed guard would have refused such rows there too, so these captures are outside what the runtime supports by design; (c) the step-544 gravity head output is wrong for this capture; (d) something else.

C. **How many captures are affected.** Without a GPU, estimate for every ARKitScenes capture among the read's 84 captures whether the same refusal is expected: from capture metadata (image size, declared rotation, sky direction if recorded), from the raw tower cache (the tower's own poses for the reference frame), or from the ground-truth poses of those captures if they are local. Say what could be computed and what needs the checkpoint's heads on a GPU. Give counts of items by type on the affected captures.

D. **What the direct (plain) read did with these captures.** The same development items were read without tools at steps 100 to 544. Do the ARKitScenes items show a deficit there that an orientation mismatch would explain (compare the leg, its parent and f17 on d3 against d1 and d2 for one or two types, from the step-544 packet's per-item results if they carry the dataset)? Keep this short; it is a consistency check.

E. **What to do.** The smallest correct handling in the reader, separating: recording a geometry refusal per capture and turning the capture's items into bound refusals in both arms (so one capture cannot abort the read); applying an orientation the data declares, if (a) holds, and what that would need to stay faithful to training; and anything that belongs to the next training seal instead. Say which of these change what the read measures.
