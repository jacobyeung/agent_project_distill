# End-to-end reader with in-call heads (brief 04), 2026-11-16

**Question.** Can one reader serve every call from the checkpoint's own forward (in-call towers, own heads), with no cached perception, fitted selector, alias table, depth cut or guard outside the weights, at no cost against the C8r runtime?

**Answer.** The hook, the loader and the stage-1 socket work and match the old runtime where they should. The selector head reaches parity on raw names with each name featurised alone; the learned scale head does not reach its bar, so the default scale is median(own depth / G3T depth) on the in-checkpoint depth head. K1's SAM 3 cache hides an alias table (26% of instances from alias prompts); removing it costs the logistic about 3 top-1 points.

**Operational definition of "in the forward" (ORCH 1440Z).** The hook runs in the decode loop between LM forwards, and the pinned tree formatters (code roots of the artifact manifest) print the head output in the v3.14 format the checkpoint was trained on (metres, 2 decimals) and `error: <code>` on a failed head. The model still converts units and writes its final answer itself. Moving the hook into the LM forward changes no token; replacing the printed response with embeddings needs a retrained model.

**Options rejected.** Training the selector on alias tables or on all-name exclusivity (the in-call tower prompts the raw name and sees only the names called so far). Rescuing the scale head with rules (forbidden by the brief). The sealed lifting's erosion and canvas crop and the estimator's (0.1, 10) depth gate (rules outside the weights).

**What might not work.** The in-call tower's proposal ranks may differ from the filtered alias cache (measure on one capture on GPU). The interim read locates the distance loss in the lifting (inflated boxes), not in scale or selection; lost small objects cost little because the model then answers directly. A box rule without tuned constants that resists mask-edge pixels, or the own-encoder head of brief 05, is the fix to test. 157 held-out objects make top-1 noisy. The scale head never saw 32-dense G3T output. Trinity envs lack fla, so the parity read's camera numbers can differ from job 170056 within numerical noise.

**Tests that would settle it.** Decompose the distance loss on the 50 interim items offline (old cache masks through the new lifting; new masks through the old lifting; old scale through the new boxes). The GPU parity read on the 636 dev-S1 items (arms own-depth scale and scale head, raw-name selector, no depth cut) against job 170056, paired by room; EAT-1 on the fixed code.

**Shipped bestspread differs from the priced one in two documented ways: camera-gap eligibility and the fewer-than-four-points case.** Both box the same points (best frame only, finite points in front of the camera, spread at most the frame median). The shipped `lift_bestspread` keeps lift_plain's `frames` (every frame with lifted points); the priced variant sets `frames=[best]`. The tree's camera gap (`trace_format/box_v313.py` lines 255-257) admits an instance only when the asked frame is in `frames`, so for a camera gap at a visible non-best frame the shipped reader answers (box centre from best-frame points) and the priced variant refuses with `camera_instance_not_visible`. Second, when fewer than four points survive the cut, the priced `_keep` returns the instance as not seen (empty `frames` and points), while the shipped reader keeps it seen and `min_area_box` raises `box_needs_four_points`, which the tree prints as `box unavailable` instead of `not seen` (ground calls and object-object gaps alike; tests/test_bestspread.py::test_fewer_than_four_kept_points_is_box_unavailable_not_unseen). The K1 price covered object-object distance only, so camera gaps are unpriced under either rule; the shipped eligibility equals the pre-change reader's. Not aligned, because aligning would change the printed frame list and refuse camera gaps the trained policy issues. Codex review 2016Z (receipt `agent/scratch/codex_runs/20261116T201305Z_b04_review_bestspread`) and its round 2 found these differences and gaps in the tests; the tests now drive a real session, cover a later larger best frame and non-finite or behind-camera points, and compare against a frozen, hash-checked copy of the priced harness (`tests/data/lift_price_v2_8f78bf08.py`); five mutants of lift_bestspread and the session wiring each fail them.
