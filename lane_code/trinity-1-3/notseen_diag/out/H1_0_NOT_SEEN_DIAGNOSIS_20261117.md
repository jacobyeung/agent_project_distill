# H1-0 distance: why the own arm prints `not seen` on 42% of reads, and what fixes it (2026-11-17)

Lane notseen_diag (Devin, trinity-1-3, CPU only). `DD` = `/data2/jjyeung/agent_project_data/distill_design/`, `EV` = `/data2/jjyeung/agent_project_data/distillation_orchestrator_20260918/orch_n30/events/`, `L` = `trinity-1-3:/scratch/jjyeung/orch_n30/notseen_diag`, `PKG` = the H1-0 package payload `p446` (Orchard `okh7/e6fc152498/p446`, copied to `L/work/f3/p446`). All counts are dev-S1 routed distance reads of job 170706 (step 544, own arm, 150 baseline reads, 46 rooms, 60 captures). Intervals: room bootstrap, 10,000 draws, seed 20261115.

## Question
On 63 of 150 baseline reads a `<ground obj=... mode="box">` call returns `not seen`. Which reader stage prints it, is the object really absent, which names fail and why, why the tower arm of job 170056 does better on the same items, which fixes pay most under EAT-1, and do the 1,532 simulated recovery rows of tree build 9 match the real failures?

## Answer
1. **The selector head's null slot prints almost every `not seen`; SAM 3 finds the object.** Counted per unique (item, name) pair, 74 of 298 grounded pairs are `not seen` in the baseline (24.8% [16.6, 32.8]). They carry 112 of the 112 not-seen ground calls and, through the operand state, all 63 not-seen gap calls.

   | Stage (code that decides it) | Pairs (ground calls) baseline | x0.5 | x2 (69 reads) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | SAM 3 keeps no proposal (joint >= 0.05, cap 30) in any of the 32 frames (`b/e2e_reader/stage1.py:99`) | 6 (6) | 6 (6) | 4 (4) |
   | Every proposal below the lift score floor 0.3 (`tree/student_pilot/routed_candidates.py:99`) | 0 | 0 | 0 |

   | Eligible proposals exist (median 6 frames): the selector head's null slot wins (`stage1.py:145-147`), or the own-depth relative-spread cut removes every remaining pixel (`routed_candidates.py:82`) | 67 (105) | 87 (145) | 23 (35) |

   The status is printed by `tree/trace_format/box_v313.py:513-524` (`ground()`: `'not seen' if not entries`, `return name+': '+status`) when `E2EGroundSession._instances` returns nothing, and the gap's `not seen` by `box_v313.py:226-228` from the operand state. `_instances` is empty when `StageOneSocket.__call__` returns `[]` (`grounding.py:146-148`: no candidates, or the selector returned -1).
   The last row is the selector, not the spread cut, wherever a perturbation flips the outcome. SAM 3, the score floor, the raster, the erosion, the crop and the spread cut are all scale-invariant: spread is `(p80-p20)/p50` of depth in a ±1% stencil (`tree/student_pilot/geometry_profiles.py:174-182`), so scaling depth and translation together leaves eligibility unchanged. `link`, `scene_candidates` and `accept_features` never drop a candidate (`routed_candidates.py:152-188`, `routed_features.py:168-219`). The only scale-dependent stage that can return "none" is the selector head, whose 58 inputs include metric features (`log_extent_*`, `member_spread_m`, `dist_to_top_m`, `n_dupes_05m`, the 0.3 m occlusion slack in `reprojection`). Scaling flips 19 of the 67 pairs to seen (17 at x2 and 4 at x0.5, 2 of them at both) and turns 27 seen pairs not seen (24 at x0.5, 3 at x2); every flip is a selector decision. The other 48 pairs are not seen at every scale; the per-call records keep no candidate logits, so the selector and the spread cut stay unseparated there (test 1 separates them).
2. **At least 66 of the 74 pairs are false negatives; at most 7 can be true.** Every item's GT answer comes from both objects' annotations, so all 74 objects exist in the scene. On 67 pairs SAM 3 proposes the name with score >= 0.3 in a median of 6 of the 32 frames, and the tower arm of job 170056 boxes 66 of them from the same 32 frames. The only candidates for true absence are the 7 pairs that SAM 3 or the raster lose, all on ScanNet++: paper bag (3 items, one capture, presence 0.024), shoe rack (3, presence 0.006 and 0.078) and slippers (1, lost to the raster). No GT instance masks for dev-S1 were reachable (they sat with the 2-18 lanes), and the frame store was not located in time, so visibility in the 32 frames is a proxy, not a GT check.
3. **Names fail by dataset, presence and size, not by portrait stretch.** Not seen / grounded pairs: heater 13/15, computer mouse 8/8, paper bag 6/7, blanket 6/6, bucket 5/5, nightstand 3/4, shoe rack 3/4, shoes 3/3, power strip 2/2, radiator 2/2, slippers 2/2, keyboard 2/4, headphones 2/7, dishwasher 2/6, stove 2/13. Large furniture never fails: bed 0/23, refrigerator 0/22, tv 0/19, door 0/11, toilet 0/9.
   - **Dataset:** ScanNet++ 57/146 pairs (39%), ScanNet 9/58 (16%), ARKitScenes 8/94 (8.5%); ScanNet++ minus the others +27.9 points [+16.2, +37.9]. Per read: ScanNet++ 46/73, ScanNet 9/30, ARKitScenes 8/47.
   - **SAM 3 presence (wording):** the best frame's presence is below 0.5 on 35 of 74 failed pairs, against 8 of 224 seen pairs. Heater is the clearest case (presence 0.07 to 0.41 with instance scores of 0.9).
   - **Size:** the median area of score >= 0.3 proposals is 0.8% of the frame on failed pairs, against 2.0% on seen pairs.
   - **Alias table:** it covers 22 of the failed pairs (heater to radiator or space heater 13, nightstand to bedside table 3, plus counter, table, ceiling light, monitor, stool and chair). The other 52 failed pairs, among them computer mouse, paper bag, blanket, bucket, shoes and power strip, have no alias. Seen pairs carry aliases on 129 of 224.
   - **Portrait:** 4/28 portrait pairs fail against 70/270 landscape ones (-11.6 [-27.6, +4.5]). Portrait is not a driver; within ARKitScenes it is +8.2 points on 28 pairs.
   Presence and mask area are selector inputs (`sam_presence_members`, `log_area_*`). The pattern is therefore the null slot reacting to low-presence or small candidates. That null slot was trained to fire on K1 objects without a positive candidate (43 of 157 held-out objects, `b04_e2ehook/heads/selector/SELECTOR_HEAD_REPORT.md`).
4. **Arm comparison: the tower arm loses 7 pairs, the own arm 74, and the 67-pair gap sits entirely in selection.** Job 170056's native arm reads the same step-544 model on the same 150 items with the C8r runtime: SAM 3 `dense_aliases` cache (cap 100), logistic `routed_default_v1` acceptance, G3T depth x own-depth scale with G3T pose, interior depth cut and voteb5 boxes. It prints `not seen` on 7 of 298 pairs (2.4% [0.0, 5.9]); own minus tower is +22.4 points [+14.9, +30.1]. The tower's 7 are exactly the 6 SAM 3 losses and the 1 raster loss above, and no pair is seen by the own arm but missed by the tower.
   - **Geometry:** on pairs both arms box, the own arm's box centres sit a median 0.71 m (IQR 0.36 to 1.8 m) from the tower's, while the scale agrees (|centre| ratio median 0.96, longest side 0.97). Own pose error, not scale, separates the geometries.
   - **Split on b04's parity read** (49 shared items, 98 pairs; G3T x own-depth scale, raw-name in-call SAM 3 and the selector head; the selector build used there, run_raw or run_iso, is not verified): tower 95 seen, parity 85, own arm 73. Of the 22 tower-seen pairs the own arm loses, 10 are also lost with G3T geometry (selector head and raw-name tower against logistic and alias cache), and 12 only with own geometry.
   - **Constants:** the alias table could explain at most 22 pairs, and the depth cut and box rule only shape boxes. Cap 30 against 100 cannot remove a candidate that has score >= 0.3.

   No reader defect beyond the null was found that moves `not seen`. The raster, erosion and crop remove 1 pair. Portrait stretch does not drive the failures (item 3). Lifting cannot fail with finite positive own depth. Unit following, which this lane does not fix, caps every all-units gain: on clean reads the metre MRA is 0.414 and the all-units MRA 0.245.
6. **The simulated recovery rows teach the right continuation on the wrong objects.** Build 9's `a0_recovery` (`tree_rebuild_w/work/rows_slices/slices_final/a0_recovery.jsonl`, sha256 f5166900, 1,532 rows; read-only copy in `L/work`) has one simulated `not seen` in every row. The missed operand is the first on 769 rows and the second on 763. A row stops calling after the miss (1 ground call on 769 rows, 2 on 763, 0 gap calls) and answers the GT value "from the frames". Compared with the real failures:
   - **Names:** door 102, bed 94, tv 85, table 81 and refrigerator 72 lead the simulated misses, and together they fail on 1 of 89 real pairs. The real top names have few rows: heater 73 rows against 13 real pairs, computer mouse 0 (the set has "mouse"), paper bag 9, blanket 17, bucket 12.
   - **Datasets:** ScanNet++ 58% of rows against 77% of real failed pairs; ARKitScenes 28% against 11%.
   - **Misses per read:** real reads miss both objects on 11 of 62 failure reads; no row teaches two misses.
   - **Continuation:** after a real miss, step 544 keeps calling (ground then gap on 37 reads, gap on 19) and 33 reads hit the cap. The rows' "no further call, answer" is the missing behaviour.

## Options considered and rejected
- **Rerunning SAM 3 or the 9B prefill on CPU to recover own depth:** about 5 CPU-min per read, past the lane's 3 hours. The scale-invariance argument plus the cache replay separates every stage except selector against spread on 48 pairs.
- **Treating the alias table as the cause:** it covers only 22 of 74 failed pairs, and SAM 3 already proposes 67 of the 74 under the raw name.
- **Treating the portrait stretch as the cause:** portrait pairs fail less often.
- **Raising the selector's acceptance with a tuned null bias:** a constant chosen on data outside the weights, which EAT-1 forbids.
- **Replaying (a) on stored candidate scores:** none are stored, so (a') is priced by projection (converted reads scored like clean reads) and tested by kit B1.

## What might not work, and how we would notice
- On the 48 persistent pairs the own-depth spread cut, not the null, may empty the candidates. Then (a') rescues fewer reads; B1's per-call diagnostics (candidates after eligibility, null against best logit) show it.
- (a') hands look-alikes to the model where the null was right (on K1 about 17% of objects that have a positive candidate get a wrong top-1, estimated from 60.5% top-1 and 114 of 157 positives). B1's per-call gap error against GT shows it; the projection assumes converted reads behave like clean ones.
- The dense cache is a proxy for the in-call tower (the in-call checkpoint hash was not compared here). Consistency on 224/224 seen pairs supports it.
- ReVSI's dataset mix differs from dev-S1's, so its not-seen rate and the ReVSI price move with the ScanNet++ share. M4's own-arm read counts it directly.
- The 49-item parity split rests on a selector build that was not verified.
