### 2.2 The registration: question, difference, confounds, rule, decision
1. **Question.** After SFT on GT traces that add unit, recovery and traced room rows and the missing direct families, does one checkpoint place its calls, terminate, and answer from the responses when every call is served inside the checkpoint, without losing direct types?
2. **The one difference from its comparison.** The comparison is step 544 read by the same reader build, on the same arm and the same items; the difference is R1's training. The rows are a bundle by the stack-first rule.
3. **Confounds.** Warm start with a fresh optimizer and a restarted schedule (D0's m100 carried a dip). Five row families at once, entering at different boundaries, so a difference at step 400 mixes 400 steps of the launch families with 200 steps of the later ones. No size row trains (slot SIZE-V, section 5), so a change on object size is drift or another family's effect; dev-S1 asks 78 of its 150 size items in feet or inches while the official sets and R0-dev ask in cm only, so dev-S1's size type measures unit handling as much as size. The parents of step 544 saw dev-S1's scenes, which favours direct answers there; both arms of every paired comparison share that exposure, and only R0-dev is unexposed (5, item 6). Recovery rows print simulated statuses. The 8,511 away rows of store v7 use ReVSI's away wording as a question template on non-benchmark scenes with non-benchmark labels, so no ReVSI facing gain is claimed as transfer to an unseen phrasing. G3T and SAM 3 have unaudited pretraining scenes. The depth and gravity heads keep training and set the tower arm's scale and level frame, so a camera or distance change on the tower arm can come from them. Vehicles P and N carry different auxiliary losses (N trains the scale token). If A2 enters at a passing boundary, every later comparison with step 544 includes GF-1.
4. **Pre-registered rule.** One decision is taken per boundary (steps 200, 400, 600, 800), against step 544. The harm rule and the protected outcomes are read on dev-S1; R0-dev is printed beside every number and stops nothing alone unless the pooled rule of item 4d is registered. Intervals are percentile intervals of a bootstrap over whole physical rooms, 10,000 draws, with the seed each tool pins (`f1f2.py` 17, the evaluator 20261115). The read tools take the interval level as a parameter, print the mean of seven with rooms resampled jointly, and print object size split by metric and imperial items; `room_bootstrap.py` prints one 95% interval per type today, and cont_ops owes the three changes before step 200.

   **4a. Harm rule and protected outcomes.** The leg stops and exports when H1, H2 or H3 holds.
   - **H1, types.** One of the seven direct types (route planning, counting, direction easy, medium and hard, relative distance, object size; 150 items each) has the interval of its paired difference wholly below zero. The level is 1 - 0.05/7 = 99.29% at step 200 and 1 - 0.05/28 = 99.82% at steps 400, 600 and 800. Object size is one of the seven: it is a protected outcome under H1 and has no efficacy gate, because R1 trains no size row.
   - **H2, mean.** The interval of the unweighted mean of the seven paired differences lies wholly below zero, with rooms resampled jointly over the 72 rooms that hold the 1,050 items. The level is 95% at step 200 and 98.75% later.
   - **H3, camera on the tower arm.** One of the three camera types (150 items each) has its interval, at H1's level, wholly below zero, or fewer than 95% of the 450 camera items draw a call (the call floor of the trace ruling's F3; step 544 drew a call on 1,050 of 1,050 camera reads, job 170631). Its baseline is the tower-arm read of step 544 with the pinned selector, one of the reads registered before the first export is read (4d).
   - **Protected outcomes: toward-facing accuracy and relative size.** Each is tested at H1's level: the 150 toward twins (33 rooms; step 544 answers 142) and the 150 relative-size items (53 rooms; step 544 answers 137). An interval wholly below zero is a HOLD the first time and a STOP the second. D0 m200 lost 4.0 points on the first and 12.67 on the second. Object size is printed split into its 72 metric and 78 imperial items, with relative size beside it.
   - **Protected outcome: termination on the claimed arm.** On the claimed arm with the pinned selector, at most 5 of the 150 dev-S1 distance reads (46 rooms) may end at the call cap, at every boundary; the count is printed with its room-bootstrap interval. A count above 5 is a HOLD the first time and a STOP the second. This behavioural requirement replaces recovery rows as a requirement of the leg: with the selector's best-candidate rule the claimed arm prints `not seen` on about 4.7% of reads (`DD/H1_0_NOT_SEEN_DIAGNOSIS_20261117.md`), so vehicle P may carry R1 to step 1,000 without recovery rows. The baseline count comes from the tower-arm read, which also gives the chance that an unchanged model exceeds 5.

   **What these numbers are.** Every rate in the table but the first row is a conditional simulation estimate: it holds for the noise of D0's m200 read, the exchange null and the stated loss model, and it is not a guarantee. The noise model is the D0 m200 read against step 544 (job 170320; `F200.json`, `A544.jsonl` 8347cd84 and `ITEMS.jsonl` c1c99e4d under `C/d0/readk/`): 1,050 paired items in 72 rooms, 95% half-widths of 4.3 to 7.9 points per type and 2.4 on the mean of seven, and correlations between types from -0.42 to +0.37 (mean +0.02), so the tests are close to independent. "No change" resamples whole rooms and exchanges the two arms inside each drawn room with probability 1/2; "flips" sets each item of the later arm to zero with probability loss / mean score; "shifted" moves the exchange null by the loss. Each pseudo-read (8,000 for the seven types, 4,000 for the size drift and the two protected outcomes) is scored with the tool's own interval, 10,000 inner draws, so the rates include the tool's noise at these levels (Monte Carlo error at most 0.008). The percentile interval on 33 to 59 rooms is anti-conservative: the estimates for an unchanged model sum to 0.063 + 3 x 0.023 = 0.132 over the four looks, against the nominal 0.0875, and 95% intervals on the seven types without the correction would stop it 17.9% of the time at one boundary. H3 adds a nominal 0.0107 at step 200 and 0.0027 at each later boundary for its three camera intervals, 0.019 over the four looks; its intervals and its call floor have no estimate until the tower-arm baseline exists. The correlation between looks is unmeasured; D0's m100 item file joined with m200 gives it. Applied to D0's m200 read, the rule stops on size ([-17.97, -1.44] at the step-200 level) and on nothing else (mean of seven -0.68 [-3.17, +1.60]). Each type's test detects half the time a loss equal to its Bonferroni half-width, 5.9 to 10.8 points at step 200, so the rule is a guard against large losses, not a certificate of no loss. Calibration evidence, kept by sha256: `EV/next_leg/card_fix_a3_evidence.tgz` (47b1b97ff96877c4..., the files of `CF/work/a3/`) and `EV/next_leg/card_fix2_b5_evidence.tgz` (9278860b169fc2ed..., the files of `CF2/work/b5/`).

   **Object size without size rows.** Lane size_diag expects dev-S1 size to drift by 0 to -6 points when no size row trains (guess: 0 to -3 if D0's metric decline was dose, -3 to -6 if it was drift). H1 treats that drift as a loss. At -3 it stops the leg with estimated probability 0.04 at step 200 and about 0.10 over the four looks; at -6, 0.17 to 0.21 at step 200 and at most 0.44 to 0.56 over the four looks (the sum of the per-look estimates; the looks share items, so the true figure is lower). A stop on size therefore says little about the repairs, and the split by metric and imperial items says whether unit handling or size moved.

   **4b. Efficacy gates.** Each family has an entry boundary E, the step from which its rows train (1 when the launch packet carries it), and one gate against step 544 on a fixed item set, paired, with a 95% room-bootstrap interval. The gate is read once at E + 200 and once more only after a HOLD. A population under 60 items gives a printed mark, not a gate. The minimum detectable improvement is the smallest true improvement that passes the gate with estimated probability 0.80, computed by double bootstrap on the baseline's paired room distribution: an item wrong at step 544 turns right with probability q, and an item right turns wrong at the stated back-flip rate (`CF2/work/b5/`).

   **4c. Verdict of a boundary, and the spending rule.** STOP: the harm rule fires, or a protected outcome or a gate fails for the second time. HOLD: the harm rule is silent and a protected outcome fails for the first time, or a gate due at this boundary fails or has no registered baseline; training continues for one boundary, the rows lane rebuilds the family where its rows are at fault, and the gate is read once more at the next boundary. PASS: the harm rule is silent, every gate due so far has passed and no HOLD is open. No gate gets a third read. A boundary is a passing boundary when it is a PASS and the unit gate is among those passed; A2 may enter only there. **Spending rule:** a family that is not in the leg by step 400 leaves R1's claim and is named so on the card. The leg passes step 400 only if the VSTI and facing gates have passed and no protected outcome is on its second failure; otherwise it stops at the step-400 export, at about 83 H100-hours (2.4). A restart after any STOP needs a new card.

   **4d. Before the first export is read.**
   - **Baselines.** Every step-544 read that a rule or gate needs is made under the pinned serving config and registered by sha256 before any R1 export is read: the tower-arm read with the pinned selector (450 camera, 150 distance and 36 room items; it gives H3's baseline, the capped and status counts and the exact unit count; section 4, rank 2), the VSTI dev read, the R0-dev read (its direct items and its 98 room items; ordered, lane cont_ops), and the count on the 49 ReVSI-worded twins. The dev-S1 direct read exists (`A544.jsonl`, sha256 8347cd84, job 170320) and is made again only if the reader build changes. Training starts without these reads, because step 544 is a fixed artifact and its reads do not depend on the leg. A gate whose baseline is missing when it falls due is HOLD, and its R1 items stay unread until the baseline is registered.
   - **Pooled harm rule.** R0-dev is the only unexposed set, and the dev-S1 rule sees a loss of D0's size only 0.51 to 0.72 of the time. One test per direct type on dev-S1 and R0-dev items together, with a bootstrap over all rooms and no second, separate test, therefore replaces H1's dev-S1 tests if it is registered before R1's first export is read (`EV/next_leg/20261117T1510Z_ORCH_RULING_card_v2.md`, item 3). The registration needs the step-544 read on R0-dev and the false-stop probability and power recomputed for the pooled rule, with H2 and H3 recalibrated jointly. The pooled rule is valid only if the baseline is the exact step-544 artifact (1d6eeb28); both arms are read on the same item keys with the same arm, serving pins, decoding and scoring; the physical rooms and the dataset weights are fixed in advance; and R0 exposure is audited. Pooling can dilute a loss confined to one dataset: dev-S1 asks half its size items in feet or inches and R0-dev asks none. Until the pooled rule is registered, the dev-S1 rule of 4a binds.
5. **Decision it changes.** PASS: R1 continues. At a passing boundary A2 may enter as a new pin, and the export becomes a possible base. At step 1,000 the export the init rule's tests select gets the leg's one official read, through a reader that passes EAT-1, and becomes the base for GF-1 and RL; that read is reported and decides nothing. STOP: the leg ends at that export, and a restart needs a new card, which starts from the cause. On object size: the split by metric and imperial items, because no size row trains; size moves to a head route (H1-2, size first) only under that card. On another type or on H2: the family that feeds the failing type is rebuilt. On H3: the serving pins and the camera rows are audited. On a gate, toward-facing or relative size: that family is rebuilt. On capped reads: recovery rows enter on vehicle N.
