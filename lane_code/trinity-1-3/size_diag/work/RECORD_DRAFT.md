# D0's object-size loss and the size family in leg R1 (size_diag, 2026-11-17)

**Question.** Why did D0 lose 9.53 points of object size on dev-S1 at step 200 against step 544, what do the size labels and the raster's "longest edge" measure, and may leg R1 train the size family as store v6 carries it?

**Answer.** Verdict (b): the trace form causes the loss that F1 measured, and the labels are right. D0 kept every sealed size question and answer and changed only the trace. The sealed rows state the length with its unit before the answer ("measure its 3D box; the longest edge is N cm"); D0's rows name the unit in a procedure sentence and give a bare number. All 9,778 rows ask in centimetres, so after the change no row shows a length next to any unit. By step 100 the model writes D0's template on 150 of 150 dev-S1 size items, and two failures follow:
1. **It stops answering feet and inches.** The 78 imperial items lose 19.62 points [-28.29, -10.68], and 18 of them are answered in another unit, 17 in metres or centimetres (step 544: 6 of 78). The 72 metric items hold (-0.42 [-3.96, +3.44]). This is the whole step-100 loss, -10.40 [-16.01, -5.17] on 150 items.
2. **It pastes the size template into two-option relative-size questions** and answers 21 of 150 at step 100 (19 at step 200) with a number, scored 0; at step 200 this costs 10.67 of relative size's 12.67 points.

By step 200 the imperial loss shrinks to -12.95 [-21.74, -4.00], and a metric loss appears: -5.83 [-11.50, -0.71] on the 72 metric items, a fall of -5.42 [-10.16, -1.45] after step 100 (R0-dev, centimetres only: -2.56 [-5.86, +0.71] on 293 items). That second loss arrives after the form switch was already complete, so the form at reading time does not cause it; further answer-only size rows or drift from D0's other changes do, and no CPU read separates the two.

Store v6 keeps D0's form, adds a first-seen frame to 8,357 of its 9,778 size rows and raises the dose from 8.2 to 10.81 rows per step of 128, so it removes neither failure. If R1 trains v6's size rows, I expect its step-200 read to repeat D0's loss and fire F1 with probability about 4 in 5, not 1 in 2. One option that the trace grammar allows is expected to keep step 544's accuracy: no size rows in R1, so that no row trains step 544 away from its own unit-bound habit (stated length with its unit on 150 of 150 items). It should prevent both failures; the metric decline would stay only if drift, not the size rows, causes it. The 2x ratio is a raster convention: the raster stores half extents, and every size answer is the full longest edge.

## Evidence

### Question 1: what each number measures
- **Row answers.** All 9,778 v6 size rows are ScanNet++ rows that ask in centimetres (templates "What is the length of the X, in centimeters?" 4,892; "longest dimension (length, width, or height) ... measured in centimeters" 1,261; "Of the length, width, and height of the X, what is the largest measurement in centimeters?" 1,227; others 2,398). Each carries an integer answer from the sealed P1C rows (pool r1g, `gt_canonical_trace`).
- **Thirty rows checked against the source annotation** (`work/q1_check.py`, `work/Q1_30ROWS.json`; the first 30 in sha256 order of `size_diag30|qid` among the 7,341 rows whose label is unique in the scene and whose r1313 assets are on trinity-1-3). The answer equals round(200 x max(axesLengths)) on 30 of 30. It also equals the longest full extent of the instance's own mesh vertices along the annotated box axes, rounded to whole centimetres, on 30 of 30 (largest gap 0.47 cm). The stored `axesLengths` are half extents: the stored world min and max equal 2|A|L on every axis (largest error 0.0000 m), and the vertices reach the half-extent box on every axis (largest |projection| / L = 1.000), while a full-extent reading misses by at least 0.084 m.
- **The raster's "longest edge".** The clean-room builder (`rows_a2/work/builder_cr/builder_cr/build_t1_cr.py`, line 14) doubles `axesLengths` only when `instances.json` declares `provider_axesLengths = half_extents`. All 482 ScanNet++ asset files and 40 of 204 ScanNet files (materialization_v2) declare nothing, yet store half extents: every ScanNet++ file shows the 2|A|L signature, and on the 40 ScanNet files the mesh extent along the longest axis is 2.00 L (median over 1,296 instances; 99.6% within 1.9 to 2.1). Their rasters' `obb_lengths` therefore hold half extents. `fs/retrace.py` (lines 69-73) compares each answer with 100 x max(`obb_lengths`) and accepts 1x or 2x, hence the ratio 2.0 on 1,146 of 1,158 pilot rows. TRACE_POOL_V3_20261108.md and COMPOSED_TRACES_COUNT_SIZE_ROOM_20261108.md record the same half-extent storage. A ratio of 2 is the raster's convention, not a row error.
- **Benchmarks.** The design index records no box construction for VSI-Bench or ReVSI object size. It records that both ask for the longest of the object's three dimensions (TRACE_RULING_20261115.md, line 160), VSI-Bench in centimetres, and that ReVSI's labels are frame-tied (FRONTIER_READ_20261111.md). Three readings support a shared full-length convention. Step 544, trained on full-length rows, reads dev-S1 at median prediction/truth 1.000 (150 items; 1.000 on the 42 ScanNet++ items). It scores 72.03 on official VSI-Bench object size (953 items) and 70.92 on ReVSI size (273 items), which predictions near 2x the truth could not reach. R0-dev's label is the longest side of the min-area z-up box of the instance's mesh vertices (`b10_reserve/code/gen_questions.py`, lines 135-140), a full extent; D0 reads it at median 1.04 (m100) and 1.07 (m200) on 293 items.
- **Units of the read sets.** dev-S1's 150 size items come from VSI-590K templates and mix units: cm 38, m 34, inches 47, feet 31. R0-dev's 293 items and official VSI-Bench ask in centimetres only, so F1 on dev-S1 weighs unit handling far more than VSI-Bench does.

- **Scale bias and refusals** explain none of the loss: the median ratio stays near 1, and no item fails to parse or reaches the budget.
- **Unit slips.** A slip is a prediction more than 1.6x off in the asked unit but within 1.35x of the truth in another unit. At m100 the slips are feet answered in metres 9, inches in centimetres 7, feet in inches 1, feet in centimetres 1, centimetres in inches 1; at m200 feet in metres 8, inches in centimetres 5, centimetres in inches 1. Reinterpreting them recovers 7.07 of m100's 10.40 points and 3.73 of m200's 9.53.
- **Concentration.** The step-100 loss sits in the imperial half and spreads over datasets (ARKitScenes -14.61 [-22.75, -6.97], 76 items; ScanNet++ -7.14 [-14.63, -0.43], 42; ScanNet -4.69 [-18.71, +9.00], 32). At m200 it covers every true-size range except objects over 2 m (under 50 cm -10.5, 21 items; 50-100 cm -14.2, 52; 100-200 cm -8.3, 52; over 200 cm -1.6, 25). No object class carries more than 2.13 points of either delta (40 classes; table -2.13 at m100 on 13 items, tv -2.07 at m200 on 14).
- **The metric decline after step 100.** On metric items m200 falls -5.42 [-10.16, -1.45] below m100, while imperial items recover +6.67 [-0.14, +13.70]. On the 127 items that no export slips, the share within 10% of the truth moves 41.7% (step 544), 37.8% (m100), 37.0% (m200). The answer-only rows do not pull answers toward a class prior: on 77 items whose class has at least 5 training rows (19 classes), the m200-minus-544 shift regresses on the distance to the training median with slope 0.12 [-0.20, 0.44], and the median distance to that prior is 0.124 at both exports.
- **Traces.** Step 544 writes "Step 1: find the X (first seen in ...). Step 2: measure its 3D box; the longest edge is N U." on 150 of 150 items, states the length in the asked unit on 145 of 150 (in metres with a conversion line such as "convert to feet: 1.21 m = 3.97 ft" on 4 feet items, in centimetres on 1 metres item), and answers with the last stated number on 150 of 150. Both D0 exports write D0's template on 150 of 150 ("Step 2: the question asks for the longest edge in U; I estimate it from the frames."), so no number stands beside a unit before the answer.
- **Hijacked neighbours.** Relative size has no rows in any of the three stores. Step 544 states both sizes on 150 of 150 items (91.3). D0 m100 answers 21 of 150 with a number (79.3, -12.00 [-21.33, -3.85]); D0 m200 answers 19 with a number, all scored 0 (-10.67 points), and on the other 131 states no size and picks a letter (90.1, -2.0 points). D0's counting template hijacks relative count the same way: numeric answers to 132 of 139 multiple-choice items at m100 (score 3.6), 9 at m200 (56.1), 0 at step 544 (61.9).

In VSI-Bench points: official VSI-Bench asks in centimetres, so the unit failure never reaches it; its exposure is the metric decline, 0 at step 100 and about 0.7 at step 200 (5.83 over eight tasks) if that decline transfers. The larger cost of R1 as designed is F1 itself: a size failure halts the leg and sends size to H1-2, which is not launchable. For object size these reads contradict the older finding that traces asserting unseen numbers cost about 12 points against bare answers (TRACE_RULING_20261115.md, line 129): here the stated length is worth 10.40 [5.17, 16.01] points at step 100 on the same 150 items, all of it on the imperial half.

## What might not work, and how we would notice
- **Style transfer.** Leaving size out relies on step 544's habit surviving R1's other rows, which all carry measurement-free traces (direction, relative distance, counting, VSTI relative, room). R1's direct room rows (2.00 per step) use exactly D0's bare-number form ("The question asks for the combined floor area in square feet.", then the number). Sign: at R1's m100 export, fewer than about 140 of the 150 dev-S1 size traces still state "the longest edge is N U", or imperial items fall.
- **Drift instead of dose.** The metric decline between D0's steps 100 and 200 could come from D0's other changes (fresh optimizer, restarted schedule, about 50,000 re-rendered direction rows) rather than from more size rows. Then R1 without size rows keeps the imperial items but can still lose about 6 points on metric items. Sign: R1 m200 loses metric size while its traces keep step 544's form.
- **Units.** dev-S1's unit mix comes from VSI-590K templates; the design index does not record ReVSI's size units, so the unit failure's reach on ReVSI is unknown.
- **R0-dev.** R0-dev has no step-544 read, so D0's R0-dev size (57.30 at m100, 54.74 at m200, 293 items) has no pair. R1's stop rule needs that read.
- **Raster consumers.** Any program that reads `obb_lengths` from the rows_a2 or K1 rasters of the 482 ScanNet++ scenes and the 40 undeclared ScanNet scenes reads half extents and must double them. The v6 3D-box rows are unaffected: their rule is the min-area z-up box of the mesh vertices.

## Tests that would settle it
1. **R1's own m100 and m200 reads (no extra cost).** On dev-S1, count the size traces that keep step 544's form and compare metric and imperial items with step 544 separately. Imperial items holding confirms the form diagnosis; metric items falling while traces keep step 544's form identifies drift.
2. **Step 544 on R0-dev** (R1's rule needs it anyway). It pairs D0's R0-dev size reads and gives the centimetre-only loss on unexposed rooms.
3. **A forced-template read of step 544** (its think block prefilled with D0's size template; dev-S1 150 size items; one H100, about 0.3 H100-hours plus a prefill option in the read kit) would measure the template's cost at reading time without training. D0 m100 already gives that answer for metric items (-0.42 [-3.96, +3.44]), so this read is optional.
