**The conflict I found in the sample rows**
- The facing and counting rows are rendered as tool-calling programs. Our measurements say programs lose to direct answering by 8 to 28 points on those types with the masks we deploy, which is why the last leg routed only an allowlist.
- The alternative in the last leg was no better: its direct rows for those families have empty think blocks, which breaks your rule against answer-only traces.
- A third issue sits underneath both. Program rows are trained on responses from ground-truth instances, while at inference the responses come from predicted masks.

**What I did**
I started `trace_ruling` (Fable on Devin) to establish the facts per row family and argue four options:
1. Keep the allowlist and give direct rows qualitative, non-empty traces.
2. Route more types.
3. Train program rows on the responses the deployed pipeline actually returns, keeping only rows whose trace can honestly reach the answer, so the model learns item by item when to trust its own perception.
4. Use a prompt flag.

I think option 3 is the most promising because it attacks the measured grounding bottleneck directly, but it needs pricing first. No GPU is in use yet.
