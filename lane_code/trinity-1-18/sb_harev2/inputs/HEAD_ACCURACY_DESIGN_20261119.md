# Head accuracy inside the checkpoint: which head could beat the direct answer, per category, and how three pricing kits decide it (2026-11-19)

Lanes sb_headacc and sb_headacc2 (Devin Fable xhigh, trinity-3-30). Status: design done; three kit specifications written; no GPU used. `B` = `distillation_orchestrator_20260918/orch_n30`. Kit specifications: `B/events/sb_headacc/KIT_SPEC_{box_ladder_k1,tower_frames,object_route_r0dev}_v2.md`. Per-item evidence, row sets and the reader source the kits read are staged on trinity-1-18 under `/scratch/jjyeung/n30_mirror/sb_headacc2/` (`pack/`, tar sha256 73951ed8...012a; `K1_ROWSETS/`), with copies on trinity-3-30 `/scratch/jjyeung/orch_n30/sb_headacc2/out/`. "Type points" are points on a type within its dev set; benchmark points divide by 8 (VSI-Bench) or 7 (ReVSI), by the mapping of `R1_M200_TRACE_ANALYSIS_20261119.md` section 11. Numbers are dev proxies (dev-S1, R0-dev) or K1 training-room numbers, except the historical evidence on official benchmark items that the Evidence section sets apart: that evidence is sensitivity evidence only and neither selects a rule nor prices a gain here.

## Question
For every category that loses points at step 200 of leg R1: is there a head inside the checkpoint (an own head, or a frozen tower shipped in it) whose printed value could beat the model's direct or current routed answer, priced without training, and how would we know? For distance and camera: what do the printed gap and the printed camera motion get wrong, and which paired test attributes the loss?

## Answer
1. **The checkpoint has no object, and no measured upside exists yet for building one.** The `<ground>` call returns a fragment. On the 142 step-544 distance gaps the selector chooses among four or more linked candidates of the one name for 204 of 284 operands; the chosen candidate holds a median of 3 frames while SAM 3 proposes the name in a median of 9; 76 chosen candidates hold one frame; and the box is fitted to half of one frame's pixels (`lift_bestspread`). An object module (merge a name's fragments into objects, each with its frames, one fused box, a first frame, and a count of objects) would print what distance, size, relative distance, appearance order and counting need. No such module exists, and every program priced on predicted masks so far lost to or tied the direct answer, so its gain is unmeasured. The routes served through heads today are the three camera types and absolute distance, where the head's 55.5 beats the model's direct 46.5 on the same items (trace analysis section 5).
2. **How the kits decide.** K1 train rows choose every program rule, screen and selector variant, with cross-fitted selection so that a chosen rule is tested on rooms that did not choose it. K1 held-out rooms are read once, after the decisions are frozen, and decide nothing. R0-dev decides route against direct (the reserve set registered for that; the orchestrator's ruling of 04:04Z and order of 05:46Z name it): each category's bar is written before any number, each category is read once on one arm frozen beforehand, and nothing is re-selected after the read. No official item is read.

4. **What is observed about the printed gap; none of it is an isolated cause.**
   - On K1 held-out rows with GT masks and corrected labels, GT geometry scores 91.99 and the deployed geometry 75.05: GT geometry improves GT-mask distance by 16.94 points. This contrast changes the whole geometry under one box protocol; it does not isolate fusion or the single view.
   - With stored labels, the deployed pipeline answers 214 held-out rows at 60.4 and GT masks score 68.0 on 216: the means differ by 7.59 points on different denominators, unpaired.
   - On dev-S1 (step 544), gaps whose selector margin is under 1 logit score 50.0 (60 items) against 70.4 (82); gaps with fewer than three grounded frames on an object score 55.4 (93) against 73.9 (49); gaps under 1 m score 39.5 (21 items, 17 overestimated). These are correlations between a flag and a score. They do not measure a recoverable gain.
   - Only paired contrasts on identical rows attribute a loss to instance choice, masks, fusion or geometry. Kit 1 prints them: A1 - A0, A2 - A0, best A3 - A2, `gtgeo_fused` - `gtgeo_single`, `gtgeo_single` - A2.
   - Bounded elsewhere: 227 of 227 printed gaps equal the box-to-box distance (trace analysis section 10), so the closest-point rule is not at fault; a ground-truth scale adds at most about 3 points over rule S4 on K1 (`E3_BACKEND_GEOMETRY_PRICING_20261116.md`); DA3 and Pi3X do not beat G3T under one view on held-out K1 (73.38 and 71.02 against 75.05).
5. **Camera: the pattern is consistent with a noise floor on short baselines, pending a controlled test.** Displacement errs by 6 cm at the median, and 55% of its lost points sit on the 33 items whose truth is 0.25 m or less (55.5; error 4 cm). Rotation errs by 1 to 2 degrees at every size, and 52% of its lost points sit on the 25 items under 5 degrees (23.6). The reader feeds G3T the item's 32 frames; K1's stored poses come from 64. Kit 2 Part A is the controlled test: the same anchor pairs and truths on K1 train, G3T at 32 against 64 input frames, with short-baseline bands. DA3 has the better K1 pair-median rotation error (1.78 against 2.72 degrees). Camera types carry no VSI-Bench or ReVSI weight.
6. **Candidates the orchestrator named.** 64 or 94 tower frames: rank 3, as tower frames only; the language model at 64 frames is lane sb_f64's question. A second ground call under a minimum of frames: rejected as a call form; its price is kit 2's `flagged` row. A robust closest-point rule: the rule is not at fault. A stronger depth tower: kit 1 reprices DA3 and Pi3X under the fused box as a report. Selector without the null slot: a head retrain, priced apart in kit 1. SAM 3's video tracker as the source of instances: arm `track` of kit 3, chosen on K1 train before its R0-dev pass.
7. **Scale of the whole: scenarios, not a sum.** Ranks 2 and 3 change the same distance rows, and the camera gain appears once (rank 9). Kit 2 counts the box gain as (32 frames, object box) minus (32 frames, today's box), the added frame gain as (64 frames, object box) minus (32 frames, object box), and reports their joint gain once, on identical rows. Until that table exists, +0.7 to +1.6 VSI-Bench and +1.4 to +3.9 ReVSI is the envelope of rows 1 to 3 if none overlapped, not an estimate. The selector retrain is training and is not in it. Ranks 4 to 7 have no priced gain; each is bounded only by its category's loss. None of this closes the distance to the frontier models.

## The object set (design of rank 2, shared by ranks 4 to 7; fixed math, a new rule chosen on K1 train)
1. **Input:** the linker's accepted candidates of one name, each with at most one proposal per frame.
2. **Merge:** two objects merge when the overlap of their 10 cm voxel sets over the smaller set (`routed_candidates.overlap`) is at least `tau`, and in every frame they share their masks coincide (intersection over the smaller mask at least 0.5); two masks of one frame that do not coincide are two instances. Pairs merge in order of overlap until none qualifies; a shared frame keeps the larger mask. `tau` is 0.15, 0.25 or 0.40.
3. **Fuse:** each member frame gives its `bestspread` points; the box rule is one of five (today's best frame; points in 5 cm voxels that two frames support, with or without the best frame; all frames; all frames trimmed at the 2nd and 98th percentile).
4. **Print:** for a single-object call, the object that holds the selector's choice: its frames, first frame and fused box, in the present response format. For a count, the number of objects of the name (all, or those with a detection score of at least 0.5).
Kit 1 chooses `tau` and the box rule on K1 train by cross-fitting. Every K1 operand name has one GT instance, so K1 can measure a split object and cannot measure two instances merged; the count is therefore untuned and stays a diagnostic.

## Options rejected and why
- **A second ground call under a minimum of frames:** needs new rows and a new call form, and prints what an always-dense tower prints.
- **A point-cloud closest-point rule:** the label is a box rule and printed gaps already equal the box distance.
- **Another depth tower now:** no tower beats G3T under one view on held-out K1; a second tower costs 0.14B to 1.4B parameters.
- **Scale work:** bounded near 3 type points on K1.
- **A fallback to the direct answer on a low-coverage flag:** lowers the callers from 54.2 to 35.9 (trace analysis 10.1).
- **A presence head for appearance order or counting:** priced and failed.
- **Choosing rules or routes on K1 held-out, dev-S1 or official items:** K1 train chooses; R0-dev decides routes once.

## What might not work and how we notice
- **The object set may not beat the direct answer on predicted masks.** SAM 3 never proposes about a fifth of operand instances on dev-S1 (224 of 1,056 targets on 93 captures, `MASK_PROPOSAL_RECALL_DEVS1_20261112.md`), and merging recovers none of them. Kit 3 shows it per category with one read each.
- **Merging may join two instances.** K1 cannot show it; kit 3's counting rows on R0-dev do, as a diagnostic.
- **Fused boxes may inflate under tower depth.** Kit 1 prints the longest edge against GT and the cross-fitted gain; a rule that does not gain 3 points on train is not adopted.
- **K1 rooms are training rooms for the trunk and the selector,** so a K1 PASS licenses a reader build and a paired read, not a claim.
- **Kit 3 lifts with scale rule S4, a different treatment from the reader's own-depth scale.** Kit 1 measures the difference on K1 train; kit 3 runs the size read only if S4 does not handicap size there, and labels every metric row.
- **The tracker may fail on frames 3 to 10 s apart,** or not load: its first GPU minute is the check, and K1 train decides whether it replaces the linker.
- **64 tower frames may not move G3T or the boxes.** Kit 2 gates G3T's input length and the added views separately.
- **The heads move during training** (88 of 88 responses changed between steps 544 and 200, trace analysis section 3): prices hold for the export they were read on.
- **Dry-run parities prove that code matches stored outputs.** They do not show that any rule helps; only the bars do.
