# Mask quality for the ground call: the +17.2 is the wrong object or no object, not the mask's outline; four candidates, three kits (2026-11-19)

Lane sb_maskdesign (Devin Fable xhigh, trinity-3-30). Status: design done; three kit specifications written; no GPU used, nothing built or launched. `B` = `distillation_orchestrator_20260918/orch_n30`. Kit specifications: `B/events/sb_maskdesign/KIT_SPEC_mask_{phrase,sam3ft,carry}.md`. All numbers are K1 training captures under the served geometry (1,037 distance rows, 209 captures, 192 rooms; 2,074 row operands, 770 distinct objects, 64 names), refusals scored 0. "Type points" are distance MRA points on those rows; benchmark points divide by 8 (VSI-Bench) or 7 (ReVSI). Scenario gains are my estimates and are marked as such.

## Question
The box ladder (`BOX_LADDER_K1_TRAIN_20261119.md`) prices raster masks at +17.2 [+13.5, +20.9] over the deployed ground call (56.8 to 74.0) and fusion on raster masks at 78.6. How does the checkpoint get per-frame masks of named objects close to that, which candidate pays most per GPU-hour, and what does each unlock for size, appearance order and counting?

## Answer
1. **The gap is object identity and recall. Outline quality is worth nothing.** On the 456 rows where the served mask reaches IoU 0.5 with the raster on the frame the ladder prices, for both operands, the deployed call scores 75.0 and raster masks 75.4. Rows whose worse operand sits on another object carry 13.0 of the 17.2 points (287 rows, 27.4 against 74.3) and refused rows 2.9 (36 rows); rows whose worse operand is a weak or badly framed mask of the right object carry 1.2 together. Lane sb_maskfail's interventions agree: repairing frames with no overlapping proposal gives +8.2 [+5.6, +10.8], adding frames the chosen track omits gives +11.1 cumulative, and replacing correct masks by rasters gives +0.1. No candidate below therefore trains a better mask decoder.
2. **Of the 15.9 points on wrong-object and refused rows, 9.9 sit where SAM 3's pool never holds the object and 6.0 where it does.** The raw-name pool (joint floor 0.05, cap 30) holds an IoU 0.5 mask of the operand in some visible anchor for 87.0% of operands. On 175 rows it lacks one (proposal recall, 9.9 points); on 148 rows it holds every missed operand and the selector or linker loses it (6.0 points; the ladder's oracle selection, +4.1 [+2.1, +6.2] on 883 rows, is the same loss counted on accepted candidates). ScanNet++ carries 13.6 of the 17.2 points.
3. **The pool misses are SAM 3's presence score and the dataset's vocabulary, not a cut-off.** In 92% of the visible frames where the raw name keeps no candidate, the detector holds a query with conditional score of at least 0.5 and the presence score vetoes it (presence under 0.05 on 82%); the reader keeps score x presence >= 0.05. `heater` alone carries 3.8 points (65 rows, 19.1 against 79.7; ScanNet++ calls a wall radiator a heater), and the alias proposals already stored for K1 (`radiator`, `space heater`) hold an IoU 0.5 mask for all 63 heater operands the raw pool misses. Stored aliases raise pool recall from 87.0% to 90.8% of operands (heater 63, coat hanger 8, chair 4, bookshelf 3, table 1) and lose none; the other misses (rack, shoe rack, stool, kettle, file folder, dishwasher, telephone, basket and 25 more names) have no stored alias that recovers them.
4. **`wrong_instance = 0` in sb_maskfail's first table is structural, and the wrong-object cases sit in other kinds.** Every K1 operand name maps to one raster instance and equals its raster class label on all 2,074 operands, so no second instance of the same label exists for rule 5 to find. The rules also test `no_proposal` and `omitted_selection` first, and a track on another object is usually absent from the frames where the target is visible: 665 of the 2,393 omitted frames belong to wrong-object operands, and on the priced frame those operands read `wrong_class` (67%), `other` (21%), `merged` (9%). Read the frame table as "where masks are missing", never as "identity errors are 2.4%": by operand, 15.4% sit on another object and 1.7% are refused. K1 cannot measure confusion between two instances of one name; only R0-dev and dev-S1 can.

## The four candidates
**1. Phrase and retention choices.** Part 1 (CPU now): per name, the pool is the raw-name proposals or raw plus stored alias proposals (cap 30 by score). Rooms are split into the ladder's five folds; a name takes aliases when, on the fit rooms, more of its objects have an on-target priced frame with aliases than without (at least five fit objects, else raw). The priced rows come from the held-out fold through the unchanged ladder. The dictionary was written before K1 and re-enters only through this per-name test, as the ruling of 06:07Z requires for inherited choices. Part 2 (0.6 GPU-h): one SAM 3 pass over the K1 train anchors adds two pools, priced the same way: candidates kept by conditional score when the raw name keeps none in a frame, and phrases for names without a stored alias, written from the name list alone and hashed before the pass. An alias table is a constant outside the weights under EAT-1, so a phrase choice ships as rows in which the model writes the chosen phrase in `<ground obj=...>`, or inside candidate 3's weights; a retention rule ships as a declared reader constant. The kit prices and ships nothing.
**2. Carry.** The served track's best frame seeds SAM 3's tracker, which runs forward and backward over the capture's 64 dense frames; the masks at the 32 anchors replace the track. A second run seeds from the raster's largest frame and separates the tracker's quality from the seed's identity. The kit measures member precision against the 90% bar, frame recall, first-seen order, the fused box through the ladder's frozen fusion rule, and size. It reuses sb_kobj4's tracker pass (`out/object_route/K1_TRACK.json`) if that exists.
**3. SAM 3 detector fine-tune.** The presence score vetoes the detector on the dataset's names, and the presence and score heads are small and trainable, so the fine-tune puts candidate 1's gain inside the weights and adds what a table cannot: ranking among look-alikes. Queries are raster class names per anchor frame: the name's visible instances as targets, names of the capture absent from the frame and vocabulary names absent from the capture as negatives (presence 0). Sibling labels (electric heater beside heater, mattress and blanket beside bed) are the hard negatives the deployed pool confuses. Trainable: LoRA rank 16 on the fusion encoder and decoder, the presence head and the scoring head; the vision encoder, the text encoder and the mask head stay frozen, which keeps the user's frozen-tower ruling for both encoders. Two room folds: each model is priced only on rooms it never saw. The final shipped weights would train on all eligible rasters after the K1 verdict.
**4. Own head.** Finding 1 removes the requirement the pilots failed: the own encoder does not need mask-grade features, only the object's box. The head becomes a name-conditioned box and presence head on the frozen ViT taps, trained on raster boxes; the frozen SAM 3 tower turns the box into a mask through its box prompt. Its K1 test is the same ladder with the own boxes as prompts, cross-fitted by room, against candidate 3. Data: raster boxes of the 239 K1 training captures for a pilot; the 2,897 raster scenes outside K1 for a full run, whose frames would need a governed stream first. GPU: at most 4 A6000-hours for the pilot (fail-fast after 1: on held-out rooms the top box reaches IoU 0.5 with the object at its largest visible anchor for at least half of the objects), 30 to 60 for a full run. Bar: the own-box arm minus A0 at least +3.0 with a paired lower bound above 0, two room folds. It earns the stack's R0-dev look only by beating candidates 1 and 3 on K1 train. Because the head returns every instance of a name as a box, it is also the natural source of counts. It waits for candidate 3's verdict, because both need the same raster loader and the fine-tune tells us how far 239 captures go.

## Unlocks
- **Size.** On K1 train the longest edge scores 52.4 from the deployed box, 60.6 from one raster frame and 71.6 from the fused raster box. Size needs the carried frame set on the right object: candidates 1 and 3 fix the object, candidate 2 supplies the frames.
- **Appearance order.** The deployed linker orders 76.6% of operand pairs (bar 90%). The pool's best candidate reaches the first visible frame on 68% of operands, so selection cannot pass the bar; tracking backward from a confident frame is the route.
- **Counting.** K1 operands are single-instance, so K1 cannot price counting. The fine-tune kit reports, as a diagnostic on raster classes with two or more instances, how often the number of linked objects above score 0.5 equals the raster count; sb_countdesign owns the route.

## Options rejected
- **Fine-tuning SAM 3's mask decoder, or any boundary loss.** Correct-mask replacement is worth +0.1 and partial masks +0.3.
- **A larger cap, or a lower floor on score x presence.** The pool at cap 100 has the same recall as at cap 30, and the teacher pass adds nothing; the veto is the presence factor, which candidate 1's retention arm and candidate 3 address.
- **Tiles or more anchors for recall.** dev-S1 priced tiles below aliases, and failed operands are visible in a median of 6 anchors.
- **Gathering fragments by 3D overlap.** Member precision 72 to 73% at every threshold; the served depth is a 51 x 51 grid per frame, which I take to be too coarse for small objects (not tested).
- **A new selector here.** The 6.0 selection points belong to the selector refit (sb_selrefit2); candidate 3 changes the scores it reads, so its kit prices both the deployed selector and the top-score rule.
- **Training the own decoder longer on teacher labels.** Its targets are SAM 3's own proposals, which miss the same objects.

## What might not work, and how we notice
- **Aliases and vetoed queries may add look-alikes**, above all on frames where the object is absent. The 456 both-good rows must not lose more than 1 point (bar `phrase_safe`), and the kit counts masks served on raster-absent frames (976 today).
- **K1 vocabulary may not be the benchmarks'.** The gain sits on ScanNet++ names; the R0-dev look reports by dataset.
- **239 captures may overfit the detector.** Fold-held-out pricing shows it; the fail-fast compares held-out objects only.
- **The tracker may drift across cuts of several seconds.** The raster-seeded run isolates it; member precision below 90% closes the object arms again.
- **Fixed objects may still be boxed badly.** Rows with both operands good score 75, the ceiling of every candidate without fusion; scale and pose errors remain.
- **My categories use thresholds (IoU 0.5, 0.1, precision 0.2) chosen once, before looking at scores.** sb_maskfail's interventions, built independently, give the same ordering.
