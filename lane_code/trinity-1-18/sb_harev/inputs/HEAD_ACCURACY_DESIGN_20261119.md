## Question
For every category that loses points at step 200 of leg R1: is there a head inside the checkpoint (an own head, or a frozen tower shipped in it) whose printed value beats the model's direct or current routed answer, priced without training, and by how many benchmark points? For distance and camera: what limits the printed gap and the printed camera motion, and which changes inside one checkpoint recover the most?

## Answer
1. **One missing module limits five categories: the checkpoint has no object.** The `<ground>` call returns a fragment, not an object. On the 142 step-544 distance gaps the selector chooses among four or more linked candidates of the one name for 204 of 284 operands; the chosen candidate holds a median of 3 frames while SAM 3 proposes the name in a median of 9; and 76 chosen candidates hold one frame. The box is then fitted to half of one frame's pixels (`lift_bestspread`). A head that merges a name's fragments into objects, each with all its frames and one fused box, would print what distance, size, relative distance, appearance order and counting all need: a box, a first frame and an instance count. No such head exists. So today only the distance route beats the direct answer (55.5 against 46.5 on the same items), and room is the one new route whose training-free price could (table below).

3. **What limits the printed gap, in order of size (K1 held-out, corrected labels, and dev-S1).**
   - **Geometry under a single view: about 17 type points.** With GT masks the deployed geometry scores 75.05 and GT geometry 91.99. The box comes from one frame, so it is partial: boxes print 0.77 to 0.79 of the true longest edge, 19% of boxes are under 0.3 m long, and gaps under 1 m are overestimated on 17 of 21 items (39.5 MRA). On GT geometry a single best view costs 8.8 points against fusing all frames; fusing was rejected on 2026-11-11 under the own depth head (per-frame scale log-sd 0.077), and I found no price for a fused distance box under G3T (0.021 to 0.044), where that record's price list puts the per-frame scale cost of a voted box near 2 to 4 points.
   - **Instance choice and fragments: about 8 type points.** K1's deployed pipeline answers at 60.4 against 68.0 with GT masks (unpaired, stored labels). On dev-S1 a selector margin under 1 logit goes with a gap score of 50.0 (60 items) against 70.4 (82), and fewer than three grounded frames with 55.4 (93) against 73.9 (49). The two flags overlap, and both mark fragments.
   - **Grounding coverage across frames: part of the above, and the lever for it is tower frames.** Of 224 operand instances SAM 3 never proposed on dev-S1, 74 have at most two usable views in 32 frames (worth +8.8 type points if granted; strict arm, cache of 2026-11-12).
   - **Not limits:** the closest-point rule (227 of 227 printed gaps equal the box-to-box distance, and the label is a box rule); scale (a ground-truth scale adds at most 3 points; the best single scale adds 0.8 to 1.8); the depth tower (Pi3X -1.7 / +1.3 and DA3 -4.0 / +1.5 against G3T with the own scale, inside noise); the null slot (4.7% `not seen` under the best-candidate rule already pinned).
4. **What limits the printed camera motion: G3T's noise floor, on short baselines.** Displacement errs by 6 cm at the median (4.2% relative above 0.5 m) and loses 55% of its points on the 33 items whose truth is 0.25 m or less (55.5 MRA; error 4 cm on a 16 cm truth). Rotation errs by 1 to 2 degrees at every size and loses 52% of its points on the 25 items under 5 degrees. Scale shows no bias (median ratio 1.00 to 1.02). Halving every error would give 91.7 on displacement (now 82.3) and 83.2 on rotation (now 75.6). The reader feeds G3T the item's 32 frames; K1 priced it on 64. DA3 has the better K1 poses (rotation 1.78 against 2.72 degrees at the pair median). Gravity is not shown to bind. Camera types carry no VSI-Bench or ReVSI weight.
5. **Candidates the orchestrator named.** 64 or 96 anchor frames: rank 3, as tower frames only, which needs no training; the language model at 64 frames is lane sb_f64's separate question. A minimum of grounded frames with a second ground call: rejected as a call form, because an always-dense tower gives the same response with no new rows; its price is kit 2's arm restricted to flagged objects. A robust closest-point rule over fused points: the rule is not the limit; the fused box is rank 2. A stronger depth tower: none beats G3T under the single view; kit 1 reprices DA3 and Pi3X under the fused box from predictions already on disk. Selector without the null slot: inside rank 2, bounded by kit 1's oracle rung.
6. **Scale of the whole.** The routes with evidence behind them (room, the distance box, tower frames) are worth about +0.7 to +1.6 VSI-Bench points and +1.4 to +3.9 ReVSI points if they pass, with no training. Size, relative distance and appearance order add only if the object call works, up to about +4.5 / +3.1 at their ceilings. None of this closes the distance to the frontier models; the object module of item 1 is what the larger categories wait on.

## The object call (design of rank 2, shared by ranks 4 to 7)
Fixed math after SAM 3 and the selector, inside the call; every constant is one the tree already uses, declared for the EAT-1 review.
1. **Seed:** the selector's best candidate, as today.
2. **Gather:** every other proposal of the name at or above the linker's score floor joins the seed when at least a quarter of its 10 cm voxels lie in the seed's voxels (the linker's own overlap rule), repeated until no proposal joins. Each frame contributes at most one proposal, the one with the largest overlap.
3. **Fuse:** in each member frame keep the `bestspread` points; keep the 5 cm voxels that two or more member frames support, plus the best frame's points (the vote-plus-best-view rule of 2026-11-11). With one member frame the rule equals today's.
4. **Print:** frame list of all members (first frame = first seen), box by `min_area_box` on the fused points. The response format does not change, so no row changes.
Kit 1 chooses among fusion rules on K1 train and takes one held-out look; the selector is retrained on K1 tables without the null slot as a separate rung.

## Options rejected and why
- **A second ground call under a minimum of frames:** needs new rows and a new call form, and prints what an always-dense tower prints. Kept only as kit 2's flagged-object arm, a latency option.
- **A point-cloud closest-point rule:** the label is a box rule; GT masks and GT geometry under the box rule score 92 to 93.
- **Another depth tower now:** inside noise under the single view; a second tower costs 0.14B to 1.4B parameters. Revisit after kit 1's fused-box arm and the room kit.
- **Scale work:** bounded at 3 type points.
- **A fallback to the direct answer on a low-coverage flag:** lowers the callers from 54.2 to 35.9 (trace analysis 10.1).
- **SAM 3's video tracker in place of the linker:** 32 frames are 3 to 10 s apart; unpriced and it needs temporal order the reader does not give it. Reopen at 64 or 94 frames if gathering fails.
- **A presence head for appearance order or counting:** priced and failed (32.9 against 71.3; 24.8 with GT presence).
- **Direction and route through boxes:** GT-mask programs already trail direct.

## What might not work and how we notice
- **Fused boxes may inflate under tower depth,** as they did under the own depth head. Kit 1 prints the longest edge against GT (today 0.79 at the median; inflation shows as above 1.2) and stops the rule at a paired loss on K1 train.
- **Gathering may pull in look-alikes.** Kit 1 scores gathered members against GT instance masks; a member precision under 0.9 fails the rule.
- **K1 rooms are training rooms for the trunk and the selector,** so K1 selects program rules only; the claim comes from kit 3 and a paired dev-S1 read.
- **64 tower frames may not move G3T** (job 170056 and the in-call tower already agree at 32). Kit 2's first arm is the 32-against-64 pose and depth comparison on K1, at 0.5 GPU-h, before anything else runs.
- **The heads move during training** (88 of 88 responses changed between steps 544 and 200): prices hold for the export they were read on; repeat kit 3's CPU stage on each milestone's scale.
- **My expected points are estimates.** The bars in the kit files, written before any number, decide.
