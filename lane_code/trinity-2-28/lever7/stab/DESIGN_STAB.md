### Pricing the tower stability check on dev items (STAB) (registered 2026-11-21, lane lever7, before any score; ORCH ANSWER 2026-11-21T23:40Z; facts trinity-2-28 /scratch/jjyeung/orch_n30/lever7/stab/FACTS_STAB.json, scripts stab/work/)
- Question: if the routed reader trusts a tower call only when the default and deterministic tower paths agree, and answers an untrusted call some other way, does it score more benchmark points on dev-S1 than always keeping the tower's answer? CPU only, on banked reads. Every sentence about the check carries its basis: separation shown on 16 audited captures (10 gross, 6 pass).

**Threshold**
- No usable rule without a free parameter exists. The tower's own first-pass repeat bound is T1's: 17 later rows of scannet/scene0515_00 differ from its first pass by 0.043 deg (largest per-frame rotation) and 0.0022 m (camera centre; 0.00186 m by HISTORY_DIAG's element metric). The default and deterministic paths disagree by more than that on 84 of 84 K5 captures, and by more than twice it on 74. K5's own repeat (A1 against A2) is bit-identical, a bound of 0 that flags all 84. Either rule would distrust almost every call.
- Disagreement: A1 against D1, the largest per-frame rotation difference in degrees (score_k5.compare). It is scale-free, so it is valid on all 84 captures, including the 24 whose K5 scale is a unit-plane stand-in. Translation is not used.
- Fit set: K5 captures outside the 38 audited ones that have GT cameras bound at the 32 anchors, never any of the 38. These are 21 rank-5 NOGT NPZs (lever1_c/arch5 and arch6 tars; 41254810 is left out, as rank 5 excluded it) and 19 camera captures with T1-GT NPZs (cont_ops25_c/cam/gt/t1_gt/gt). Together they make 40 captures: 21 ScanNet, 13 ScanNet++ and 6 ARKit. Frame indices bind on all 41 files. Five captures (scene0261_03, 0320_00, 0320_02, 0687_00, 623c973c56) have no GT file on Trinity and stay out.
- Label: gross means that A1's rigid-fit rotation maximum against GT exceeds 60 deg (the audit's rule and fit code, gt/v2.alignment). The label is scale-free. Pass and moderate need a metric scale, which the 19 new captures lack, so they are not used to fit.
- Rule: tau maximises Youden's J (share of gross flagged minus share of non-gross flagged) on the 40. Each cut sits at the geometric mean of two adjacent sorted disagreements. A tie goes to the larger tau, which flags fewer captures. If the 40 hold fewer than 3 gross or fewer than 3 non-gross captures, the fit is void and tau = 3.9 deg. That value is K5's 90th percentile of the disagreement, read on all 84 captures without ground truth (RESULT 2301Z). A call is untrusted when its capture's disagreement exceeds tau.
- Reported beside, not used to choose: how many of the 38's 10 gross and 6 passing captures tau flags (an out-of-sample check), and the same flags with A1's translation added.

**Items** (all of dev-S1's 786 tower-arm reads lie on K5's 84 captures, so no GPU minute is needed to complete dev-S1)
- Camera types (ScanNet and ScanNet++ only; ARKit has no camera item): 150 items each. Displacement covers 51 captures (28 ScanNet, 23 ScanNet++), rotation 49 (29 / 20) and direction 48 (26 / 22), 75 items per dataset. Routed distance has 150 items on 60 captures: ScanNet 30 items on 11 captures, ScanNet++ 73 on 19 and ARKit 47 on 30.
- Flagged captures by tau, then flagged items for displacement / rotation / direction / distance. At 0.25 deg: 40 captures, 19 of them audited; 41 / 47 / 61 / 97 items. At 1.0 deg: 23 captures (12); 20 / 21 / 30 / 47. At 3.9 deg: 9 captures (4); 2 / 6 / 10 / 16. The 3.9 deg camera items sit on only 2 rooms per type.
- Decidable type: at least 10 flagged items in at least 3 rooms, and a ceiling (100 x flagged items / 150) of at least 2 points. Below that, the type is reported as too few to decide. At 3.9 deg the ceilings are 1.3 / 4.0 / 6.7 / 10.7 points, and a count-only minimum detectable effect (80% power, item difference sd 0.5, items in a room fully correlated) is 1.3 / 4.0 / 6.7 / 6.2. Only distance could decide there.

**Reading and bars** (benchmark points on the type's official scorer; paired room bootstrap, 10,000 draws, seed 20261115; all 150 items per type, where unflagged items differ by 0)
- Worth building: for some decidable type and arm, swap minus baseline is +1.0 point or more, with the lower bound of its 99.375% interval above 0 (Bonferroni over 2 arms x 4 types). The bar of 1.0 point is twice the 0.5-point noise rule of ANSWER 1902Z. The check is then built for that type with that arm only; every other type keeps the tower's answer.
- Closed: on every decidable type, both arms' 95% upper bounds fall below +1.0. Undecided: anything else. Too few: no type is decidable.
- Build on a pass: a reader switch that runs the deterministic tower pass in a sidecar process. The sidecar is needed because CUBLAS_WORKSPACE_CONFIG and the deterministic switches must be set before CUDA starts, and setting them in the served process would move the default path. The switch compares rotations with tau and routes the type's untrusted calls to the passing arm. It then gets one review, a CPU check and one served dev-S1 confirmation read with the check on (about 4 H100-hours, 171253's size). This is a serving-pin change, so R1's step-544 camera baselines are read again under the card rule.
- On closed: nothing is built, K5-S stays a diagnostic, and the instability goes to the tower-repair queue as evidence.
- On undecided or too few: nothing is built. Dev-S1 has no more camera items; more would need new camera items built from ScanNet and ScanNet++ GT. Only distance can grow, on R0-dev (290 items, 98 ScanNet++ captures). The two-path tower there takes about 26 H100-minutes, plus about 10 minutes of Orchard staging, load and warmup and a CPU check of about 25 minutes. It also needs tower-arm and own-arm reads of about 2 H100-hours each (the direct read exists, 171197). That spends the unexposed set, so it needs a ruling.
- Serving cost: two tower passes per capture. The default pass takes 7.3 / 7.3 / 4.3 s per capture on an H100 (ScanNet / ScanNet++ / ARKit) and the deterministic pass 8.5 / 8.5 / 4.8 s, about 2.15 times the tower. On dev-S1 that adds about 605 s to a 3.7-hour full read, about 5%. The own heads already run in every read (each 171253 row holds heads.pose9), and declining a call costs no extra pass.

**What might not work, and how we notice**
- The fit set is thin and mostly ScanNet, so it may hold few gross captures. FACTS records the void-fit fallback, and the out-of-sample count on the 38 shows a bad tau.
- The served default path drifts with process history (up to 0.14 m and 1.4 deg on ARKit), so served flags can differ from K5's. We report fragile flags (disagreement within a factor of 2 of tau) and the price with those flags flipped.
- The sidecar deterministic pass may not reproduce K5's D1. We test it during the build with bit equality on 10 captures against the K5 return.
- Arm (i) is not a direct answer, so a direct-answer arm needs a checkpoint that answers without calling.
