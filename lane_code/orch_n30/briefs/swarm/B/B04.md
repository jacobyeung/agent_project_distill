## Goal and why this task matters
We are training a ~9B spatial VLM, end to end from RGB, whose chain of thought calls heads inside its own checkpoint and answers from what they print; it must beat open 9B models on ReVSI, VSTI and VSI-Bench and later serve as a cheaper base for navigation fine-tuning. A training leg is one 24-hour Orchard job on 4 H100 that spends about 3.5 h before its first step and then reaches only about 450 steps at about 133 s a step, and the reads that judge each boundary take 4 to 6 h on one GPU slot, so every minute removed buys more steps or more experiments. A boundary's development reads take 4 to 6 h on the one Orchard general slot we may hold, so reads, not training, often decide how fast we learn whether a leg helped.

## Question
Measured: A4 read 636 items in 7,352 s (11.6 s per item), distance items 23.6 s mean with one 648-s item, reader load 75 s. Where does per-item time go (frame decode and vision encoding of 32-64 frames, prefill, generated tokens, tower calls), and how many items per minute would each lever give: reuse of vision features or of the frame prefix's KV cache across questions on the same scene, a cache of tower results per (scene, frame set), generation caps and runaway cutoffs, item ordering by scene, and scene-balanced sharding across preempt slots? Batched greedy decoding was rejected earlier because it is not bit-identical; any lever must keep each item's tokens identical or say plainly that it does not.

## Method
1. From the corpus, compute per read and per question type: items per scene, output-token distribution, share of items at the cap, tool calls per item. Use these to model time per item as load + per-scene encode + per-item prefill + tokens x per-token time + tower calls, and fit the constants to the measured A4 and distance timings; write the model and fit to files/B04/time_model.py and a table.
2. Read the harness to see what is recomputed per item that depends only on the scene (frames, processor outputs, vision features, tower outputs), whether the prompt places frames before the question (which makes the frame prefix reusable), and what max_new_tokens and stop rules apply.
3. Patch the best lever behind a flag (for example, a per-scene cache of processor outputs or of vision features) and prove token-for-token equality of greedy generation with the flag ON and OFF on a tiny randomly initialised model of the same architecture class on CPU.
4. Write files/B04/gpu_parity.py: the 50-item GPU parity check the orchestrator can run in one Orchard job.

## What counts as a finding, and its price

Price as GPU-hours per boundary of reads. CONFIRMED needs the CPU equality test or a corpus statistic you computed.
