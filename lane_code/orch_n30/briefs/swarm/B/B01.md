## Goal and why this task matters
We are training a ~9B spatial VLM, end to end from RGB, whose chain of thought calls heads inside its own checkpoint and answers from what they print; it must beat open 9B models on ReVSI, VSTI and VSI-Bench and later serve as a cheaper base for navigation fine-tuning. A training leg is one 24-hour Orchard job on 4 H100 that spends about 3.5 h before its first step and then reaches only about 450 steps at about 133 s a step, and the reads that judge each boundary take 4 to 6 h on one GPU slot, so every minute removed buys more steps or more experiments. Job 172660 took 209 min from submission to step 201, and 141 min of that was mode p, a CPU preparation that runs while 4 H100 sit idle: about 9.4 GPU-hours per leg, the largest single waste we measured.

## Question
Of the 209 min from submission to step 201 (job 172660: import preflight 1.2, four full checks of the 113.5 GB checkpoint 11.3, model/store/archive staging 7.6, frame staging and verification 23.9 (f3 alone 18), mode p 140.6 = start to result 70.4 (parent metadata 5.4, pointing ~38.6, VQA ~2.2, routing ~23.9) plus result to exit 70.2 (run.prepared, frame inventory, SQLite reference counts, SHA samples), second-stage prep 2.4, binding 10.9, first step 7.2, plus a review wait of up to 120 min at WAITING_REVIEW), which work (a) recomputes outputs whose inputs do not change between legs, (b) could run once in a CPU job or before submission, or (c) could run faster inside the allocation with byte-identical outputs on 48 CPUs?

2. For each mode p substage, find the hot loops: repeated sha256 over the same bytes, single-threaded loops over rows or frames, SQLite inserts without one transaction, per-row fsync, files read more than once, JSON re-encoding.
3. Build a synthetic fixture in your task directory (a small SQLite store and fake frames shaped like the real ones, no real data) and time the hot functions with cProfile before and after each patch. Show byte-identical outputs (sha256 of every produced file) with the switch ON and OFF.
4. Price the 120-min review wait and the 11.3-min checkpoint re-checks separately, and propose the cheapest safe change for each (for example, a review that runs before submission on the CPU prepare-only output, or a verification memo within one allocation).
Two ideas are already known not to work today: a node-local prepared-input cache never hits in production, and a cross-run preparation store is blocked because the preparation identity includes run paths. Revisit them only if you find a cheap path-free identity, and then say exactly which fields change.
