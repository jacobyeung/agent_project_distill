## Goal and why this task matters
We are training a ~9B spatial VLM, end to end from RGB, whose chain of thought calls heads inside its own checkpoint and answers from what they print; it must beat open 9B models on ReVSI, VSTI and VSI-Bench and later serve as a cheaper base for navigation fine-tuning. A training leg is one 24-hour Orchard job on 4 H100 that spends about 3.5 h before its first step and then reaches only about 450 steps at about 133 s a step, and the reads that judge each boundary take 4 to 6 h on one GPU slot, so every minute removed buys more steps or more experiments. The main thread waits a median 31.7 s of each 132.6-s step for rows that are read from SQLite, audited and encoded synchronously, one row per microstep, 72 microsteps per rank; that is about 4 h of wall time, or 16 GPU-hours, per 450-step leg spent waiting on data.

## Question
Per row, what does the CPU do between SQLite and the forward pass (query, JSON decode, audit, frame decode, resize, processor and tokenizer encoding, collation, device copy), which of it repeats across rows of the same scene or the same template, and which host-device syncs and logging calls sit inside the step loop? How long do validation reads at exports take?

## Method
1. Build a synthetic fixture shaped like the real row store (a small SQLite file with rows of the real schema, fake JPEG frames at the real resolution) and a tiny processor or the real processor class with a dummy tokenizer, and cProfile one rank's 72-row step on CPU. Write the cost table to files/B07/ROW_COSTS.md.
2. Find avoidable work beyond the three known patches: frames decoded more than once per step, resize at train time that could be done once at preparation, re-tokenizing the same prompt template, per-row SQLite connections or unprepared queries, synchronous metrics writes, .item() or synchronize calls in the loop, validation work that blocks training ranks.
3. Patch the top two or three behind switches with a CPU test that the fetched (stream, index, candidate) sequence, the encoded tensors and the collective order are identical with each switch ON and OFF; give the expected seconds per step.

## What counts as a finding, and its price
A finding names per-row or per-step work that need not happen synchronously or at all, its code location, the CPU time measured on the fixture, and the expected seconds per step on the real job. Price as seconds per step x 450 / 3600 x 4 = GPU-hours per leg. CONFIRMED needs the CPU profile and a parity test.
