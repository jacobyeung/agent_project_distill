# F06: RL stage scaffolding, rewarding correct and short reasoning: audit rlkit-v1's reward and rollout store on real development traces, and harden them

Read /data2/jjyeung/agent_project_data/distillation_orchestrator_20260918/orch_n30/briefs/swarm/SWARM_RULES.md first. It binds you, and so does the file it names first.

## Goal and why
The project builds a ~9B spatial VLM that answers from RGB and calls heads inside its own checkpoint in its chain of thought. The user's plan is SFT that forces head-using reasoning, then RL that rewards correct and short reasoning. A kit (rlkit-v1) already exists, CPU-tested and reviewed, but its reward has never seen real model traces, and its only copy sits on one node. Your job is to replay its reward on the 32 real development reads, find where it would reward the wrong thing, and deliver tested fixes, so the RL stage waits on nothing once an SFT export is chosen.

## Method
1. Run the kit's existing tests in your copy, one module at a time; report the counts.
2. Write replay_reward.py that maps each corpus arm row to the kit's reward inputs (c from score, n from output_tokens, path from whether tool calls exist, g as far as the slim fields allow; say which gate checks cannot be replayed from slim fields and treat them as pass, marking that). Use the kit's default lam, n_ref and n_min rules, or a per-type n_min from the 25th percentile of correct-arm lengths on a training-only subset if FLOOR files are absent; state which.
3. Report, per question type, on development items only: mean reward by arm; the share of correct rows the length term penalises and by how much; whether the reward ranks B2048 above full where both are correct; rows where a correct answer with no head call outscores a correct head-using answer on a family whose program should call a head (this is the user's goal inverted: RL must not teach the model to drop its heads); runaway rows (hit_cap) and their reward; parse failures.
4. Look for reward hacks in the formula and gates: a short guess that scores c near 1 on MRA families with wide tolerance; a head call whose printed value is ignored but whose answer is right; multiple-choice items where option priors give high c. Write each as a unit test that fails today if the hack exists.
5. Rollout store: test crash and resume (a draw half-written, a duplicate draw id, two writers), and that the store never loses or double-counts a draw. Fix what fails.
6. Deliver fixes as `diff -ruN` against the staged copy, each behind a config key whose default keeps today's behaviour, with tests.

## What counts as a finding
A replayed case where the reward would push the policy away from head use, toward guessing, or toward runaways; a store fault you reproduced. CONFIRMED needs the test or replay row.

## Price
Per type, the reward gap that drives the wrong preference, and the share of training prompts (by type mix of the development reads) it touches; benchmark points are unpriced until an RL round runs.

## Out of scope
Sampling, GPUs, the GRPO trainer patch (grpo_tree is not staged), new reward families, official benchmark items.

## Orchestrator review before landing
Every default-off fix and the replay table. The reward decides what RL trains, so the review by the other model family is required, plus a check that the kit's HASHES.txt is updated for changed files.
