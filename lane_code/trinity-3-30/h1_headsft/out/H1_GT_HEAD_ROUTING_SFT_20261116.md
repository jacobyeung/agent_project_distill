# H1: does SFT on ground-truth head-calling traces make the student call, train and use its own heads? (design, 2026-11-16)

Lane h1_headsft (Devin, trinity-3-30). Lane folder `L=/scratch/jjyeung/orch_n30/h1_headsft`. `DD` = `/data2/jjyeung/agent_project_data/distill_design/`, `EV` = `/data2/jjyeung/agent_project_data/distillation_orchestrator_20260918/orch_n30/events/`, `T` = the sealed trainer tree b01ce449 (`/scratch/jjyeung/orch_n30/tree_rebuild/work/base/tree_b01ce449` on trinity-3-30). "Own arm" = every call served by heads inside the checkpoint. "Tower arm" = brief 04's reader (frozen G3T in the call, scale head, own gravity). Numbers marked "guess" have no measurement behind them.

## Question
When the 9B student is trained by SFT on ground-truth traces that say exactly where its chain of thought calls a head, and every call is served by a head inside its own checkpoint, does it (1) call the right head at the right point, (2) carry heads accurate enough to answer from, and (3) use what the head prints? What is the smallest experiment that answers all three, what does it cost, and what does it do to ReVSI official999?

## Answer
1. **Types, heads and positions.** Four types route; the rest answer directly, as the trace ruling and the room ruling fix (`DD/TRACE_RULING_20261115.md`, Answer 1-6).

   | Type (benchmark) | Call, at the GT trace's position | Own head behind each printed field | GT source of the training response |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | camera displacement (VSTI, dev-S1) | after the plan sentence: `<move from=i to=j>` → `dx, dy, dz, distance`; then "the printed distance is…" and the answer | pose head (pose9 translation, metric), gravity head (level frame) | GT trajectory of the row's 32 frames (`gt_pose9`, `gt_up`) |
   | camera movement direction (VSTI, dev-S1) | same call; then the four option scores and the argmax | pose head, gravity head | same |
   | camera rotation (dev-S1; no VSTI type) | `<turn from=i to=j>` → `turn = ±N deg`; then the answer | pose head (rotation), gravity head | same |
   | object absolute distance (ReVSI, VSI-Bench) | `<ground obj=A mode="box">`, `<ground obj=B mode="box">`, `<gap a=A b=B>`, one conversion line written by the model, the answer | SAM 3 inside the artifact (masks) + selector head; depth head and pose head lift the mask to a box in frame 1's level frame; gap is fixed math | GT instance masks, GT depth and GT poses (the sealed v3.14 rows) |
   | room size, counting, size, relative distance, direction, appearance order, route planning | no call (direct trace) | — | — |

   Gravity is served inside every call (level frame of move, turn and the box yaw), so H1 adds no gravity-only call; no benchmark type needs one. Head-form rows for the direct-majority types (counting, relative distance, direction medium and hard) belong to b08's both-path rows and enter H1 only after b08 admits them.
2. **Two steps, measurement first.** **H1-0** reads checkpoints that already exist with the own arm: step 544 and D0's m200 export. It costs about 5 H100-hours per checkpoint and no training, and it answers all three questions for the SFT the lineage has already had. **H1-1** is the smallest SFT leg: D0's exact leg (init step 544, D0's rows, seed 17, D0's 1,000-step LR schedule) stopped after step 200, plus two switches, S1 and S2 (item 3). D0's own m100 and m200 exports are its control, because they share init, rows, order and schedule. H1-1 costs about 38 H100-hours on Orchard's four advanced H100 (200 steps at about 140 s), plus about 10 H100-hours of reads.
3. **The two switches.**
   - **S1, faithful own-value camera rows.** Scheduled sampling draws own head values for camera routing rows with p = 0.5 from step 1. The response is re-rendered from own values, and the scoring lines, decision and answer that follow are re-derived from the printed own values by derivgen's faithful materializer (`T/generator/derivgen_v7/derivgen/materialize.py:494`). The whole suffix trains. Today an own-valued row trains nothing after its first response (`T/student_pilot/carrier_c1_routing.py:443-447`), so own values have never taught the model anything, and D0 draws none (`max_own_probability 0.0` in kit `07ca49df/k/b02e4385.json`). Distance rows stay GT-valued, because `rerender` refuses grounded rows by design.
   - **S2, pose targets on camera routing rows.** The pose stream's camera loss also runs on each camera routing row's own 32 frames, from the `gt_pose9` and `gt_up` that derivgen's `camera_rows.py` writes into every clip. The pose and gravity heads then train on the videos they serve.
4. **Rows and mix.** H1-1 keeps D0's 288 rows per step at world 4: pose 64 (194,987 rows), VQA 128 (D0's receipt, every direct type), routing 64 (47,732 rows: camera 35,423, distance 11,728, room 581), pointing 32 (20,239) (`DD/ARCHITECTURE_CURRENT.md` section 4). Over 200 steps it draws about 9,500 camera rows (about 4,750 own-valued under S1), 3,100 distance rows and 25,600 direct rows. No unit or recovery rows enter, because D0 has none and the pairing needs equal rows; H1-1b adds A0's 1,532 recovery and 2.9 unit rows per step and gives up the pairing.
5. **Init: step 544.** The init rule binds the next leg (A2 or A0), not H1 (`DD/NEXT_LEG_INIT_RULE_20261116.md`, Question). Step 544 is the only init with a same-recipe control already training (D0, from the same artifact 1d6eeb28), a native routed read (job 170056) and a ReVSI direct read (job 170037). A base init is rejected: its pose head would start from scratch, and a scratch pose head sat at 86 degrees at m200 (`DD/SELF_ROUTING_DESIGN_20261108.md`, standing evidence).
6. **Frames: 32 now, 64 as a parameter.** Rows, configs and the evaluator take the frame count as a parameter. The trainer and reader refuse 64 today, because the carrier readout needs exactly 32 carriers (`T/student_pilot/carrier_c1.py:590`), `OwnGeometry` checks a [32, 9] pose (`carrier_c1_routing.py:224`) and the grammar checks frames 1..32 (`p3_grammar.py:27`). 64 frames costs: about 1.8 times the step time (about 4,160 to about 8,300 visual tokens per row; guess from token counts, `ARCHITECTURE_CURRENT.md` section 6), so about 70 H100-hours for H1-1 and about 9 per checkpoint read; a pose head with 64 carriers; and a frame convention for camera questions, which name "frame i of 32" and cannot be reworded on benchmark items. ReVSI and VSI-Bench object questions name no frame and take 64 frames directly.
7. **The three measurements and the benchmark read, per checkpoint** (step 544, D0 m200, H1-1 m100 and m200):
   - **M1 placement.** On the routed dev-S1 items (450 camera, 150 distance) and ReVSI's distance items: the call rate; the opener, frame arguments, object names and order against the call sequence the GT trace prescribes for that question; whether the answer precedes the first call; repeated or extra calls. On the dev-S1 direct items of D0's stop-rule read (1,525) and ReVSI's 679 direct items: the false-call rate. **Pass:** call rate at least 98%, exact sequence at least 95%, false calls at most 2%.
   - **M2 head accuracy.** Per call: the printed value against the item's GT (move distance against GT displacement, turn against GT rotation, the argmax option against the GT option, the printed gap in metres against the GT distance). Per frame, where GT arrays exist: rotation and translation-direction error, translation ratio, gravity angle, FoV error, depth AbsRel. Paired against the tower arm on the same items (bootstrap over rooms, 10,000 draws, seed 20261115). **Adequate for a type** when the own arm's task accuracy is within 5 points of the tower arm (interval lower bound above -5). **Heads train well in H1-1** when no per-head error at m200 is worse than D0 m200's beyond noise and the own arm's camera accuracy is not below D0 m200's.
   - **M3 usage.** The reader perturbs a head's output inside the forward before it is printed, and the model continues on the perturbed text: translation scaled by 0.5 and 2 (displacement), translation rotated 180 degrees about gravity (movement direction), turn negated (rotation), the whole scene scaled by 0.5 and 2 (distance; depth and translation together, so every printed metric distance scales). **Follow rate** = share of decision-changing items whose answer equals the program's decision on the perturbed printed values (numbers within 5% after converting the expected value into the question's unit; the model's text is never converted). **Pass:** follow at least 0.8 and stick at most 0.1 per type. A GT-substitution arm, where GT arrays exist, gives the accuracy ceiling the heads leave.
   - **M4 ReVSI official999, seven categories.** The five direct categories through b07's build R (paired against step 544's 679 items), absolute distance (283 items, every one asked in metres) through the own arm, room (37 items) direct with any room call unserved and counted. It is the first seven-category number of this lineage; step 544's read covers five (`DD/REVSI_DIRECT_STEP544_20261115.md`). ReVSI asks for the "direct distance" where the training rows say "distance", so M1's call rate on these 283 items tests wording transfer (item file `b07_offread/in/kit_revsi/inputs/i1.json` on trinity-3-30).

## Why (evidence)
- **The trainer already carries the machinery and never used it.** The decode loop serves calls from the own pose, gravity and depth heads by default (`T/student_pilot/carrier_c1_decode.py:142`, `pose_source='own'`) and has a donor-shuffle mode (line 40). The trainer has per-head own-value gates (`carrier_c1_routing.py:533-581`). The sealed leg and D0 run with the gates closed (`max_own_probability 0.0`), and every routed read serves camera calls from G3T or a cache (`DD/VSTI_NATIVE_CAMERA_READ_STEP544_20261116.md`; brief 04's scope table). No record measures the own arm of step 544.
- **Placement already looks right; usage looks strong; the heads are the open question.** Step 544 calls on all 450 dev-S1 camera items and answers them at 82.3, 94.7 and 75.7 when G3T serves the call (`DD/NATIVE_ROUTED_READ_DEVS1_STEP544_20261115.md`, table). It copies the printed metres even when a question asks for centimetres (same record, distance rows), which is usage, misapplied. The direct types hit the token budget, the mark of an unserved call, on 7 of 1,339 items (`DD/TRACE_RULING_20261115.md` 2.2). At T0 the own pose head scored about 53 and 84 on VSTI-450 camera displacement and movement direction, against 77.2 and 100 for G3T with an own-depth scale (`DD/SOTA_PLAN_20261109.md` line 17), and it missed its own training rows by 13 to 14 degrees (`DD/POSE_PLATEAU_DIAGNOSIS_20261109.md`).
- **D0 is a free control.** D0 (Orchard 170111) trains from the same artifact with the same rows and seed and exports m100 and m200 (`EV/cont_ops/STATUS.md`); H1-1 differs from it only in the two switches, so any change at m200 belongs to S1 and S2.
- **Faithful labels teach use; GT labels do not test it.** On a GT-valued row the printed value and the frames agree, so the row cannot tell whether the model reads the value. On an own-valued row with faithful labels the answer follows the printed numbers, so only a model that reads them fits the row (`DD/SELF_ROUTING_DESIGN_20261108.md` 1.2). The perturbation read tests the same property at inference without training.

## Options considered and rejected
- **A full 1,000-step H1 leg:** five times the cost, and m200 already answers the three questions against a control at the same step.
- **H1 inside A2:** saves a leg but confounds every head measurement with GF-1, which feeds G3T features into the language model; the switches are config-only and can ride in A2 after H1-1 reads.
- **G3T as the camera head in H1's primary arm:** it is a frozen external network, so it cannot show that the student's heads train or serve; it stays as the tower arm, the comparison H1 prices.
- **Perturbed-GT training rows for usage:** no measured perturbation model; own values are the real noise (`DD/SELF_ROUTING_DESIGN_20261108.md` section 2). Perturbation is a measurement only.
- **A gravity-only call or depth_at rows for distance:** no benchmark type asks for gravity alone, and the sealed distance grammar is ground/gap since v3.12; both would need new rows and a new reader path for no measured gain.
- **Base init:** scratch heads (Answer 5). **64 frames first:** 1.8 times the price before 32 frames have answered the questions (Answer 6).
- **A separate H1 reader:** the one-reader order (`EV/b04_e2ehook/20261116T1149Z_ORCH_NOTE_one_reader.md`); H1's evaluator is a perception variant, a perturbation hook and scorers over brief 04's reader core.

## What might not work, and how we would notice
- **The own pose head is too weak for faithful rows.** S1 would then train wrong answers. The leg logs the share of own rows whose faithful answer differs from the GT answer; above 35% over steps 1 to 50, H1-1 stops and reports (registered here).
- **The materializer refuses the leg's camera rows.** Its templates must reproduce every camera row's target from GT values. The kit's identity test runs on fixture rows; the real check runs on the routing receipt during kit preparation and must reach 95%, or S1 trains too few rows to read.
- **Usage already passes at step 544.** Then S1 changes nothing measurable in M3, and the finding is that GT-only SFT already teaches use; H1-1 then reads mainly S2 and own-value exposure.
- **Perturbations look implausible.** A scaled scene can print distances no room holds, and the model may then fall back to the frames. Both scale directions and the magnitude-preserving 180-degree rotation are read and reported separately.
- **The selector head misranks raw names on benchmark scenes.** Brief 04's head matches the logistic selector on K1 held-out raw names (top-1 60.5 against 59.9, `DD/E2E_READER_B04_20261116.md`) on only 157 objects. M2's per-call gap error separates grounding misses from depth and pose error, because the per-call record keeps the selected mask.
- **The selector head sees shifted inputs.** It was trained on K1 candidates lifted with G3T geometry, and its inputs include geometric features; lifting with own depth and pose changes them. The read records, per distance call, which candidate each arm selects from the same SAM 3 masks, so the selection agreement between the own and tower arms measures the shift. If agreement is low, the own arm's distance number is a grounding result, not a depth result, and the report says so.
- **No GT arrays for dev-S1 frames on Trinity.** Per-frame M2 then waits; per-call M2 needs only the items' GT answers and always runs.
- **S2 overloads the pose head.** It adds about 48 pose-supervised rows per step to D0's 64. Pose loss and the per-frame errors at m100 against D0 m100 show it; H1-1a (S1 only) is rendered beside H1-1 for that case.
- **D0 stops at m200 under its own rule.** Its m200 export still exists and still serves as the control.
- **Units:** H1-1 trains no unit rows, so non-metre dev-S1 distance questions keep failing; accuracy is read on the metre subset (43 of the 150-item list). ReVSI asks every distance in metres, so its number carries no unit loss.

## Tests that settle it
- **CPU, before any GPU (kit, this lane):** OFF identity, the first step of all four ranks bit-equal to the sealed tree with the switches off; faithful identity on camera fixture rows; S1 and S2 unit tests; the evaluator's scripted copier (follow 1.0) and ignorer (stick 1.0); placement scorer cases; every rank's first batch through the full H1 forward on a tiny model; then the real step-544 export's first batch on a CPU node that holds the full export (trinity-1-18's `k1/m544_parts` if it is complete; otherwise the first-step admission of the Orchard job).
- **H1-0 on step 544:** M1, M2 (own against tower) and M3 on dev-S1; M4. Registered reading: if M1 and M3 pass and the own arm is within 5 points of the tower arm on a camera type, the own heads can serve that type now.
- **H1-1 against D0 m200:** M1 to M4 paired. Registered reading: S1 and S2 help if M3's follow rate and the own arm's camera accuracy are not below D0 m200's and the per-head errors are not worse; any direct type whose paired interval lies wholly below zero is harm (D0's F1 rule).

## Registration (before any H1 read or launch; ORCH GO 2026-11-16T13:42Z)
Item sets are pinned by sha256 in the kit (`L/kit/items/`): dev-S1 routed (636 items: 150 per camera type, 150 distance, 36 room), dev-S1 direct (the D0 stop-rule set), ReVSI official999. Statistics: per-type paired differences on shared items, percentile 95% intervals from a bootstrap over physical rooms, 10,000 draws, seed 20261115; a refusal, unserved call or non-answer scores 0 and stays in the denominator.
- **M1 bars (per checkpoint, own arm).** Routed types: call rate at least 98% and exact expected call sequence (opener, frames or object names, order) at least 95% on each of the four types; no answer before the first call on more than 1%. Direct types: false-call rate at most 2% overall and at most 5% on any type. ReVSI distance: call rate at least 95% (wording transfer).
- **M2 bars.** A type is served adequately by its own heads when the own arm's task accuracy minus the tower arm's has a lower bound above -5 points. Heads train well in H1-1 when, at m200 against D0 m200: no per-call error distribution (move distance, turn, gap) is worse (paired interval on the median absolute error wholly above zero), and own-arm accuracy on each camera type has a lower bound above -2 points.
- **M3 bars.** An item enters a mode's denominator when the perturbation changes the printed decision: a different option, or a printed number moved by at least 20%. Follow = the answer equals the program's decision on the perturbed printed values (numbers within 5% of the printed perturbed value converted into the question's unit); stick = the answer equals the unperturbed run's answer (within 5%). Pass per type: follow at least 0.80 and stick at most 0.10. In H1-1, S1 helps usage when m200's follow rate minus D0 m200's has a lower bound above 0 on any camera type that failed the bar in D0 m200, and no type falls below 0.80.
- **Control comparison.** H1-1 m100 against D0 m100 and H1-1 m200 against D0 m200 on every set, same reader build and items. Harm: any direct type of D0's F1 list with a paired interval wholly below zero, or ReVSI's five direct categories with a mean difference wholly below zero. Stop rule inside the leg: if the share of own camera rows whose faithful answer differs from the GT answer exceeds 35% over steps 1 to 50, H1-1 stops at the next milestone and reports.
- **Readings.** H1-0 alone decides nothing about the next leg; it sets the baselines. H1-1 is read as above at m200 only; m100 is reported.

## H1-2 (next step, priced, not built ahead of H1-1)
Trace ruling Answer item 6 gives every type that a head inside the artifact can inform both forms. H1-2 extends H1-1's head routes to relative distance and object size through own depth and grounding (`<ground>` per named object, then a fixed-math comparison or the longest box edge), and counting through instance grounding: one `<ground>` per category that returns an instance list, then the model's own count (b11's presence probe cannot count; it answers whether, not how many).
