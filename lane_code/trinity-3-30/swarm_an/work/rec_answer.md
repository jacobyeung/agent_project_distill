## Answer

"Type points" are points on the type within its dev set. "Benchmark points" are points on the benchmark's headline mean under the mapping of section 11 (VSI-Bench: mean of eight tasks; ReVSI: mean of seven categories, each the mean of its subtypes; VSTI: mean of five subtasks). They come from dev items only and are proxies, not benchmark estimates.

1. **The answer follows the trace almost everywhere, so the loss sits in what the trace asserts.** On direction (1,028 items), size (443), route (150), VSTI (900) and the three camera types (450) no wrong item has an answer that departs from the trace's conclusion; relative distance has one departure in 131 wrong items. The traces assert the deciding relation, order or number in one line with no evidence behind it. The exceptions are appearance order, where the answer beats its own trace, and routed distance, where 15 answers depart from the response.
2. **Most of the loss is perception or head accuracy, which no rewording buys.** On metre questions, the only unit the benchmarks ask, the printed gap costs 39.8 type points (5.0 VSI-Bench points; 5.7 ReVSI points by proxy) and calls cost 0.4. Object size costs 3.8 VSI-Bench and 6.0 ReVSI points, and its answers beat the training class median by only 3 to 5 points where the class has rows. Appearance order costs 4.7 VSI-Bench points, relative distance 3.3 and 4.7, room 4.1 and 5.1. Camera displacement costs 3.6 VSTI points, all head accuracy.
3. **Four row changes carry measurable benchmark points** (section 5): totals with one instance per category (ReVSI, at most 1.6); the clause-driven half turn under both openings (ReVSI +0.4 to +0.9, at most 1.3); a counting form that cannot loop (VSI-Bench +0.5 and ReVSI +0.4 back to step 544's level on the looping items, at most 2.2 and 2.6); and back relations in direction rows (at most 0.6 on each benchmark). Centimetre, inch and feet conversion rows are worth 5.9 type points on dev-S1 and nothing on the benchmarks.
4. **Counting loses most, and the loop is only its visible part.** Runaways cost 17.9 type points (dev-S1), 19.5 (R0-dev) and 11.5 (relative count), yet these items scored only 25 to 27 MRA at step 544. The list's length follows a prior on 2: truth-3 undercounts stop at two entries on 49 of 49 traces, at step 544 too. Counting above two instances is weak at every step (truth 3: about 46 MRA; truth 5 or more: about 15). No head in the checkpoint prints instances.
5. **The camera types place every call and use every response (450 of 450 at both steps);** all their loss is head accuracy. Each type has one question wording in the dev set, so robustness to wording is untested. On metre distance questions step 200 calls on 36 of 43 items and scores 57.0 against 60.7 at step 544.
6. **On ReVSI-style away questions the opening line decides the turn, and object names decide the opening.** 43 of 202 away traces open with the toward-only skeleton and 42 omit the half turn (31 wrong). That opening appears on 20 of 53 away items whose three object names are all outside the 73 names of the rows that teach the turn, and on 0 of 9 whose names are all inside.
7. **The VSTI questions ask about objects that are mostly out of view at the queried frame.** In store v7's rows at least one queried object has no visible share at frame k on 1,112 of 1,200 sampled rows, so "locate X, first seen in frame f" is the honest line and a relation "read at frame k" cannot be. The two- to four-option camera types sit 4 to 13 points above the best constant answer, and the model's order changes with k on 18 of 59 item pairs whose true winner changes.
8. **Many traces cite frame numbers that do not exist.** Store v7 rows cite the index 1 to 32 on every sampled citation, and the input has 32 frames. At step 200 the traces cite values above 32 on 100 of 246 appearance-order items, 373 of 900 VSTI items and 121 of 150 away twins. The values are not the sampled frames' times in seconds (section 10.4), and no wrong answer is shown to follow from them.
