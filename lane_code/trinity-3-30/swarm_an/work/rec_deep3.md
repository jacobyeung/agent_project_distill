### 10.18 Relative distance: joins and margins (`deep3_reldist_join`, and my recounts)
- **Several instances of a class do not explain the errors.** dev-S1 items with a candidate class that the room's counting questions count at 2 or more score 35 of 47 against 68 of 89 for items with none (my join; the analyst's wider flag gives 78 of 102 against 32 of 48). On R0-dev the join reaches 2 or 3 of 279 items: the ReVSI-style questions name classes the room's counting questions do not count.
- **Near ties by the model's own numbers hold two fifths of the errors.** Step 544 states a metre value for every candidate (429 of 429 traces). Its margin between the two candidates nearest the asked endpoint is under 25% on 54 of the 131 items wrong at step 200 and on 51 of the 298 right ones (the analyst counts 53), so step 200 scores 51 of 105 inside that band and 247 of 324 outside it. Step 544 ranks the truth second on 54 of the 131 and first on 27.
- A vote of the three checkpoints gives 109 of 150 and 191 of 279 against 110 and 188; all three share one wrong pick on 58 items.
- The stated orders are stable within a room: two questions with one reference order their shared candidates alike on 65 of 69 dev-S1 pairs, and no pair of truths conflicts.
- Measured distances cannot arbitrate: the routed distance items give two measured candidates on 6 relative-distance items, none of them wrong.
- **What a routed form might reach (an estimate, not a measurement):** the gap head's printed value is within 25% of truth on 58 of 85 numeric responses at step 200 (median absolute log ratio 0.11). Drawing two such errors independently and applying them to step 544's stated endpoint distances orders the endpoint pair correctly on 80.2% of 349 items, against 69.5% right for the direct answer (298 of 429). The assumptions (stated distances taken as true, independent errors, no failed calls) are untested; the same reference in both gaps would correlate the errors, which helps an ordering.

### 10.19 Object size: the class carries the answer (`deep3_size_decomp`, and my recounts)
- **Replacing every answer by the model's own median for the class changes the score by +0.5 (dev-S1, 120 items in classes with three or more items) and -0.6 (R0-dev, 97 items);** my class grouping gives 61.0 against the model's 62.8 on 105 R0-dev items. The within-class variation of the answers carries about one point.
- One oracle multiplier per class, fitted on the dev truths, adds 7.6 and 10.2 points on those items (0.76 VSI-Bench and 0.48 ReVSI points over the supported classes); 22 and 26 points of loss remain after it.
- The errors are the same at every step: the log error keeps its sign on 109 of 150 and 237 of 293 items, and on R0-dev the log errors correlate at 0.89 to 0.93 between steps. The geometric mean of the three answers scores 70.2 and 60.5 against 69.3 and 58.5.
- **Small objects are the largest single stratum on R0-dev:** truths under 30 cm are 88 of 293 items, score 48.0 and lose 15.6 type points (2.23 ReVSI points) with a median error of 5.5 cm (38 cm above 100 cm, so the loss is by ratio); 23 of the 88 answers are 30 cm or more. Training size rows answer under 30 cm on 15 to 17% (447 and 497 of 3,000 in two samples) against 30% of R0-dev items and 5% of dev-S1 items.
- Units: no conversion error is confirmed at step 200 (2 of 150 answers flagged); the scored and released truths differ on 78 of 150 dev-S1 items and move the score by 0.3.
- The box head adds nothing under any rule tested on the 58 joined items: geometric mean with the direct answer 61.9, "head when the two agree within 1.5 times" 68.1, against 73.1 direct; choosing the better of the two in hindsight gives 80.5 (+0.36 VSI-Bench points). ### 10.20 Toward direction: components, the model's own angle, and consistency (`deep3_dirtw_bits`, and my recounts)
- **Components on hard toward items at step 200:** R0-dev 46 wrong of 121: left-right alone 25, front-back alone 16, both 5; dev-S1 26 wrong of 150: 11, 14, 1. Easy R0-dev: left answered right 16, right answered left 12. Medium R0-dev: a back truth is answered as a side on 11 of 17 and a side as the other side on 26 of 112.
- **The model's layout is consistent with itself.** A left-or-right answer for (stand at A, face B, target C) fixes the orientation of the triangle A, B, C, so permuted questions about one triple constrain each other. On dev-S1 the truths satisfy 262 of 264 such constraints and the step-200 answers 255 of 263 (step 544: 243); on R0-dev toward items, 14 of 14 and 11 of 14. A wrong answer therefore comes with matching answers on the other questions about the same three objects: the error sits in where the model places the objects, not in the turn arithmetic of one question. A mirrored layout would pass the same test.
- **By the model's own angle (step 544 states one on 826 of 826 toward traces), 40 of the 69 always-wrong R0-dev items lie within 25 degrees of a boundary of the answer regions** (5 of 17 easy, 15 of 24 medium, 20 of 28 hard). But R0-dev also fails far from the boundary: easy items more than 45 degrees from it score 46 of 63 at step 200 against 65 of 69 on dev-S1. Near-boundary errors cost 1.20 ReVSI points (68 items) and 0.94 VSI-Bench points (34 items).
- The counting join flags 17 of 450 dev-S1 direction items and none of the 376 R0-dev toward items, so several instances of a class explain little; neither video length nor the training vocabulary of the names separates wrong from right items.
- Label: `vsi590k_035564` and `vsi590k_035573` (d1/scene0250; door, trash bin, towel) have identical roles and opposite truths, and `vsi590k_022853` conflicts with the second; the room has two towels.

### 10.21 Is failure a property of the room? (`deep3_xroom`, and my recount `work/verify/xroom_recount.py`)
- **Rooms differ more than chance, by a modest amount.** With scores centred per type, the variance of room means exceeds its shuffle mean on R0-dev (0.0199 against 0.0112, p = 0.002; 98 rooms) and on the VSTI dev set (0.0258 against 0.0164, p = 0.002; 68 rooms). On dev-S1 the same statistic shows nothing (p = 0.53) because 16 of its 73 rooms have under five items; weighting rooms by their items gives 0.0113 against 0.0075, p = 0.008. The routed types show no room effect.
- **A room that fails one type does not fail the others.** Room-level correlations between appearance order, counting, relative distance and size on R0-dev lie between -0.12 and 0.14 (80 to 96 rooms); direction pooled over levels correlates 0.29 with appearance order. The R0-dev and VSTI scores of the 68 rooms the two sets share correlate at 0.04. Within one type a room stays as it was: step 544 against step 200 correlates at 0.58 (appearance order), 0.57 (counting), 0.69 (relative distance) and 0.76 (size).
- Half of the lost score sits in 35 of 98 R0-dev rooms and 14 of 73 dev-S1 rooms. The 20 R0-dev rooms with the lowest centred mean hold 360 items and 207.3 of 674.3 lost score units: 6.5 of the 22.4 ReVSI proxy points lost on the direct types (the analyst's 8.36 priced appearance order as a ReVSI category).
- **Long videos score lower on R0-dev:** the room mean falls with duration (Spearman -0.23, p = 0.024); the longest third of rooms (178 to 705 s, 22 s between sampled frames at the top) sits 0.04 to 0.05 below its type means and the other two thirds about 0.02 to 0.03 above. VSTI shows no such relation (0.14, p = 0.24). Distinct object names and item count explain nothing.
- Hard direction separates the 20 worst from the 20 best R0-dev rooms most (0.16 against 0.84 on 38 items each) and size least (0.57 against 0.72).
