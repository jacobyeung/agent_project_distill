## Question
For every category that loses points at step 200 of leg R1: is there a head inside the checkpoint (an own head, or a frozen tower shipped in it) whose printed value could beat the model's direct or current routed answer, priced without training, and how would we know? For distance and camera: what do the printed gap and the printed camera motion get wrong, and which paired test attributes the loss?

## Answer
1. **The checkpoint has no object, and no measured upside exists yet for building one.** The `<ground>` call returns a fragment. On the 142 step-544 distance gaps the selector chooses among four or more linked candidates of the one name for 204 of 284 operands; the chosen candidate holds a median of 3 frames while SAM 3 proposes the name in a median of 9; 76 chosen candidates hold one frame; and the box is fitted to half of one frame's pixels (`lift_bestspread`). An object module (merge a name's fragments into objects, each with its frames, one fused box, a first frame, and a count of objects) would print what distance, size, relative distance, appearance order and counting need. No such module exists, and every program priced on predicted masks so far lost to or tied the direct answer, so its gain is unmeasured. The routes served through heads today are the three camera types and absolute distance, where the head's 55.5 beats the model's direct 46.5 on the same items (trace analysis section 5).
2. **How the kits decide (orchestrator's ruling of 06:07Z).** K1 train alone chooses or rejects programs and camera changes: every program-side rule and every choice among arms (box fusion, thresholds, tracker against linker, frame count), with cross-fitted selection so that a chosen rule is tested on rooms that did not choose it. K1 held-out rooms are read once, after the decisions are frozen, and decide nothing. R0-dev, the reserve set registered to decide route against direct, sees one comparison per category: the single arm K1 chose against the pinned present path, on all items of the category, with the bar and the decision written below before the read, and one read. A category that misses goes back to K1; no new registration permits rereading the same R0 category with a changed arm. Every dev-S1 read, reader reads included, is diagnostic, and nothing read there is a claim. No official item is read. Every R0-dev read is logged in `B/events/swarm_build/R0DEV_LOOKS.md`.

4. **What is observed about the printed gap; none of it is an isolated cause.**
   - On K1 held-out rows with GT masks and corrected labels, GT geometry scores 91.99 and the deployed geometry 75.05: GT geometry improves GT-mask distance by 16.94 points. This contrast changes the whole geometry under one box protocol; it does not isolate fusion or the single view.
   - With stored labels, the deployed pipeline answers 214 held-out rows at 60.4 and GT masks score 68.0 on 216: the means differ by 7.59 points on different denominators, unpaired.
   - On dev-S1 (step 544), gaps whose selector margin is under 1 logit score 50.0 (60 items) against 70.4 (82); gaps with fewer than three grounded frames on an object score 55.4 (93) against 73.9 (49); gaps under 1 m score 39.5 (21 items, 17 overestimated). These are correlations between a flag and a score. They do not measure a recoverable gain.
   - Only paired contrasts on identical rows attribute a loss to instance choice, masks, fusion or geometry. Kit 1 prints them: A1 - A0, A2 - A0, best A3 - A2, `gtgeo_allspread` - `gtgeo_best1` (one depth cut in both arms), `gtgeo_best1` - A2.
   - Bounded elsewhere: 227 of 227 printed gaps equal the box-to-box distance (trace analysis section 10), so the closest-point rule is not at fault; a ground-truth scale adds at most about 3 points over rule S4 on K1 (`E3_BACKEND_GEOMETRY_PRICING_20261116.md`); on corrected K1 held-out labels no candidate tower differs from the served geometry beyond noise (against 75.05: Pi3X -1.7 as priced and +1.3 on the rows both answer, DA3 -4.0 and +1.5, every interval spanning 0; `TOWER_REDECISION_20261117.md`), a diagnostic that chooses nothing here.

DA3 has the better K1 pair-median rotation error (1.78 against 2.72 degrees). Camera types carry no VSI-Bench or ReVSI weight.
6. **Candidates the orchestrator named.** 64 or 94 tower frames: rank 3, as tower frames only; the language model at 64 frames is lane sb_f64's question. A stronger depth tower: kit 1 reprices DA3 and Pi3X under the fused box as a report. Selector: a refit on labels rebuilt under the serving contract is a separate kit (ruling 07:19Z, lane sb_selrefit); every pricing contrast here keeps the deployed selector. ## The object set (design of rank 2, shared by ranks 4 to 7; fixed math, a new rule chosen on K1 train)
1. **Input:** the linker's accepted candidates of one name, each with at most one proposal per frame.
2. **Merge:** two objects merge when the overlap of their 10 cm voxel sets over the smaller set (`routed_candidates.overlap`) is at least `tau`, and in every frame they share their masks coincide (intersection over the smaller mask at least 0.5); two masks of one frame that do not coincide are two instances. Pairs merge in order of overlap until none qualifies; a shared frame keeps the larger mask. `tau` is 0.15, 0.25 or 0.40.
3. **Fuse:** each member frame gives its `bestspread` points; the box rule is one of eleven (today's best frame; points in voxels of 5 or 10 cm that two or three frames support, with or without the best frame; all frames; all frames trimmed at the 2nd and 98th percentile).
4. **Print:** for a single-object call, the object that holds the selector's choice: its frames, first frame and fused box, in the present response format. For a count, the number of objects of the name (all, or those with a detection score of at least 0.5).
Kit 1 chooses `tau` and the box rule on K1 train by cross-fitting. Every K1 operand name has one GT instance, so K1 can measure a split object and cannot measure two instances merged; the count is therefore untuned and stays a diagnostic.

## Options these kits do not build, and why
K1 train alone chooses or rejects programs. No option below is rejected by a test here: each is left out of these kits for the stated reason, and every dev-S1 or held-out number quoted is a diagnostic.
- **A second ground call under a minimum of frames:** needs new rows and a new call form, and prints what an always-dense tower prints; kit 2 reports its K1 train price as row `flagged`.
- **A point-cloud closest-point rule:** the label is defined between boxes, so a point-cloud rule would answer a different quantity.
- **Another depth tower:** outside these kits' question; a second tower costs 0.14B to 1.4B parameters. Kit 1 reports DA3 and Pi3X under the fused box on K1 train, and K1 train would choose.
- **Scale work:** a ground-truth scale is worth about 3 type points over rule S4 on K1.
- **A fallback to the direct answer on a low-coverage flag:** not built: it needs the model's direct answer on each item, which K1 train rows do not have, so K1 could neither choose nor reject it.
- **A presence head for appearance order or counting:** this design proposes none; the K1 held-out numbers of `PRESENCE_PROBE_K1_20261116.md` are quoted above as a diagnostic.

## What might not work and how we notice
- **The object set may not beat the direct answer on predicted masks.** SAM 3 never proposes about a fifth of operand instances on dev-S1 (224 of 1,056 targets on 93 captures, `MASK_PROPOSAL_RECALL_DEVS1_20261112.md`), and merging recovers none of them. Kit 3 shows it per category, one comparison each.
- **Merging may join two instances.** K1 cannot show it; kit 3's counting rows on dev-S1 do, as a diagnostic.
- **Fused boxes may inflate under tower depth.** Kit 1 prints the longest edge against GT and the cross-fitted gain; a rule that does not gain 3 points on train is not adopted.
- **K1 rooms are training rooms for the trunk and the selector,** so a K1 choice is a choice of rule, not a claim; the route claim for a category is its one R0-dev comparison.
- **Kit 3 lifts with scale rule S4, a different treatment from the reader's own-depth scale.** Kit 1 measures the difference on K1 train; kit 3 runs the size read only if S4 does not handicap size there (same object set, refusals scored 0), and labels the row.
- **The selector is served a geometry it was not trained on** (tables under S4/G1, reader under the own-depth scale). Kit 1 prices every arm on the served geometry with the deployed selector; the refit on serving-contract labels is the separate selector kit's, and a row whose capture has no rebuilt label table keeps A0's own score in both arms of a label contrast.
- **The tracker may fail on frames 3 to 10 s apart,** or not load: its first GPU minute is the check, and K1 train decides whether it replaces the linker.
- **64 tower frames may not move G3T or the boxes.** Kit 2 gates G3T's input length and the added views separately.
- **The heads move during training** (88 of 88 responses changed between steps 544 and 200, trace analysis section 3): prices hold for the export they were read on.
- **Dry-run parities prove that code matches stored outputs.** They do not show that any rule helps; only the bars do.
