## Question
The box ladder (`BOX_LADDER_K1_TRAIN_20261119.md`) prices raster masks at +17.2 [+13.5, +20.9] over the deployed ground call (56.8 to 74.0) and fusion on raster masks at 78.6. How does the checkpoint get per-frame masks of named objects close to that, which candidate pays most per GPU-hour, and what does each unlock for size, appearance order and counting?

## Answer
1. **The gap is object identity and recall. Replacing correct masks by rasters moves the score by +0.11 [-0.83, +1.10].** On the 456 rows where the served mask reaches IoU 0.5 with the raster on the frame the ladder prices, for both operands, the deployed call scores 75.0 and raster masks 75.4. Rows whose worse operand sits on another object carry 13.0 of the 17.2 points (287 rows, 27.4 against 74.3) and refused rows 2.9 (36 rows); rows whose worse operand is a weak or badly framed mask of the right object carry 1.2 together. Lane sb_maskfail's interventions agree: repairing frames with no overlapping proposal gives +8.2 [+5.6, +10.8], adding frames the chosen track omits gives +11.1 cumulative, and replacing correct masks by rasters gives +0.11 [-0.83, +1.10]. No candidate below therefore trains a better mask decoder.
2. **Of the 15.85 points on wrong-object and refused rows, 9.85 sit where SAM 3's pool never holds the object and 6.01 where it does.** The raw-name pool (joint floor 0.05, cap 30) holds an IoU 0.5 mask of the operand in some visible anchor for 87.0% of operands. On 175 rows it lacks one (proposal recall, 9.85 points). On 148 rows it holds every missed operand: on 11 of them (0.28 points) only below the lifting floor of 0.3 on the conditional score, and on 137 (5.73 points) at or above it, where the linker or the selector loses the object. The ladder's oracle selection over accepted candidates, +4.1 [+2.1, +6.2] on 883 rows, is the part a selector can reach; it is a share of the 5.73, not a gain to add to it. The remainder is candidates that pass the floor and join no accepted track (sb_mfrev finding F2 counts 156 such frames beside 1,487 inside accepted tracks), rows outside the 883, and boxes that stay poor on the right object; the stored files do not separate these. Of the 9.85 points, 2.88 sit on the 36 refused rows, which score 0 here but which the model answers directly on a benchmark read, so their benchmark value is smaller than their K1 value. ScanNet++ carries 13.6 of the 17.2 points.
3. **The pool misses are SAM 3's presence score and the dataset's vocabulary, not a cut-off.** In 92% of the visible operand-frames where the raw name keeps no candidate (2,511 of 2,716 row-weighted; 814 of 891 distinct), the detector holds a query with conditional score of at least 0.5 and the presence score vetoes it (presence under 0.05 on 82%); the reader keeps score x presence >= 0.05. The vetoed queries' masks were not stored, so this shows a veto, not that the vetoed query sits on the target. `heater` alone carries 3.8 points (65 rows, 19.1 against 79.7; ScanNet++ calls a wall radiator a heater), and the alias proposals already stored for K1 (`radiator`, `space heater`) hold an IoU 0.5 mask for all 63 heater operands the raw pool misses. Stored aliases raise pool recall from 87.0% to 90.8% of operands (heater 63, coat hanger 8, chair 4, bookshelf 3, table 1) and lose none; the other misses (rack, shoe rack, stool, kettle, file folder, dishwasher, telephone, basket and 25 more names) have no stored alias that recovers them. As one written phrase per name, at the lifting floor: `radiator` reaches all 84 heater operands (`heater` 21) in a median of 4 anchors and `coat rack` all 8 coat hanger operands (`coat hanger` 0); for every other name the raw name is the best single phrase. Those two switches put 64 rows that carry 3.9 points within reach and lift pool recall from 84.7% to 88.1% (the pooled table: 89.5%).
4. **`wrong_instance = 0` in sb_maskfail's first table is structural, and the wrong-object cases sit in other kinds.** Every K1 operand name maps to one raster instance and equals its raster class label on all 2,074 operands, so no second instance of the same label exists for rule 5 to find. The rules also test `no_proposal` and `omitted_selection` first, and a track on another object is usually absent from the frames where the target is visible: 665 of the 2,393 omitted frames belong to wrong-object operands, and on the priced frame those operands read `wrong_class` (67%), `other` (21%), `merged` (9%). Read the frame table as "where masks are missing", never as "identity errors are 2.4%": by operand, 15.4% sit on another object and 1.7% are refused. K1 cannot measure confusion between two instances of one name; only R0-dev and dev-S1 can.

## The four candidates
**1. The written phrase.** An alias table inside the tool does not ship (ruling 09:49Z): the phrase is something the model writes in its own call, taught by rows, or something inside the detector's weights. Part 1 (CPU, GO given) therefore prices policies the model can write. For each name a policy is one phrase (the name or one stored alias), or an ordered pair where the second phrase is called only when the first returns `not seen`. A phrase's pool is the stored proposals of that prompt alone under the reader's retention. Rooms are split into the ladder's five folds; a name takes the policy that puts most of its objects on target at the priced frame on the fit rooms (ties to fewer calls, then to the raw name; at least five fit objects, else the raw name), and the held-out fold's rows are priced through the unchanged ladder. The dictionary was written from language alone and re-enters only through this per-name test, as the ruling of 06:07Z requires for inherited choices. The kit also reports which phrases carry the gain and how many routed rows would be rewritten: on K1 train 91 of the 1,037 rows name heater or coat hanger (8.8%), the two names whose stored alias beats the raw name alone. The pooled raw-plus-alias pool is reported as a recall count only: the reader keeps one presence per frame (`cache_for`, applied to every instance by `routed_candidates.lift`), so a pool that mixes prompts cannot be priced through it. The stored cache merged alias proposals into raw ones above IoU 0.95, so a stored alias pool understates the alias wherever the raw name also finds the object. Part 2 (1.2 GPU-h) reruns the chosen phrases alone, adds phrases for names without a stored alias (written from the name list alone and hashed before the pass), and prices retention by conditional score where the presence score vetoes, a reader constant that would need a seal review.
**2. Carry.** The served track's best frame seeds SAM 3's tracker, which runs forward and backward over the capture's 64 dense frames; the masks at the 32 anchors replace the track. A second run seeds from the raster's largest frame and separates the tracker's quality from the seed's identity. The kit measures the ladder's member precision (added members whose dominant raster instance is the seed's, over all objects) against the 90% bar, frame recall, first-seen order, the fused box through the ladder's frozen fusion rule, and size.
**3. SAM 3 detector fine-tune.** The presence score vetoes the detector on the dataset's names, and the presence and score heads are small and trainable, so the fine-tune puts candidate 1's gain inside the weights and adds what a table cannot: ranking among look-alikes. Queries are raster class names per anchor frame: the name's visible instances as targets, names of the capture absent from the frame and vocabulary names absent from the capture as negatives (presence 0). Sibling labels (electric heater beside heater, mattress and blanket beside bed) are the hard negatives the deployed pool confuses. Trainable: LoRA rank 16 on the fusion encoder and decoder, the presence head and the scoring head; the vision encoder, the text encoder and the mask head stay frozen. Two room folds: each model is priced only on rooms it never saw. The final shipped weights would train on all eligible rasters after the K1 verdict. The ruling of 09:49Z allows the adapter under the user's ruling of 2026-11-10; a checkpoint that ships it is reported as SAM 3 with a trained detector adapter, not as a frozen tower, and it does not yet ground from the model's own encoder. It gets no GPU before the phrase kit's result: if written phrases recover most of the 9.85 pool-recall points, the adapter is not the next spend.
**4. Own head.** Finding 1 removes the requirement the pilots failed: the own encoder does not need mask-grade features, only the object's box. The head becomes a name-conditioned box and presence head on the frozen ViT taps, trained on raster boxes; the frozen SAM 3 tower turns the box into a mask through its box prompt. Its K1 test is the same ladder with the own boxes as prompts, cross-fitted by room, against candidate 3. Data: raster boxes of the 239 K1 training captures for a pilot. A full run would need the raster stores outside K1 (2,897 scenes by `B12_COUNT_APPEARANCE_ROWS_20261116.md`); their scene and raster manifests and the proof that they exclude held-out, reserve and benchmark rooms are MISSING, so that expansion is not used until both exist, and its frames would need a governed stream. GPU: at most 4 A6000-hours for the pilot (fail-fast after 1: on held-out rooms the top box reaches IoU 0.5 with the object at its largest visible anchor for at least half of the objects), 30 to 60 for a full run. Bar: the own-box arm minus A0 at least +3.0 with a paired lower bound above 0, two room folds. It earns the stack's R0-dev look only by beating candidates 1 and 3 on K1 train. Because the head returns every instance of a name as a box, it is also the natural source of counts. It waits for candidate 3's verdict, because both need the same raster loader and the fine-tune tells us how far 239 captures go.

## Unlocks
- **Size.** On K1 train the longest edge scores 52.4 from the deployed box, 60.6 from one raster frame and 71.6 from the fused raster box. Size needs the carried frame set on the right object: candidates 1 and 3 fix the object, candidate 2 supplies the frames.
- **Appearance order.** The deployed linker orders 76.6% of operand pairs (bar 90%). The pool's best candidate reaches the first visible anchor on 64% of operands, so selection cannot pass the bar; tracking backward from a confident frame is the route.
- **Counting.** K1 operands are single-instance, so K1 cannot price counting. The fine-tune kit reports, as a diagnostic on raster classes with two or more instances, how often the number of linked objects above score 0.5 equals the raster count; sb_countdesign owns the route.

## Options rejected
- **Fine-tuning SAM 3's mask decoder, or any boundary loss.** Correct-mask replacement is worth +0.1 and partial masks +0.3.
- **A larger cap.** The pool at cap 100 has the same recall as at cap 30. A lower joint floor is untested (masks below 0.05 were not stored); the phrase kit's retention arm is its test.
- **Tiles and more anchors: not built here, not rejected.** Neither has a K1-train price; operands the raw pool misses are visible in a median of 6 anchors, which is why they come after the phrase and the presence veto.
- **Gathering fragments by 3D overlap.** Member precision 72 to 73% at every threshold; the served depth is a 51 x 51 grid per frame, which I take to be too coarse for small objects (not tested).
- **A new selector here.** The 5.73 points at or above the lifting floor belong to the selector refit (sb_selrefit2) and to the linker; candidate 3 changes the scores it reads, so its kit prices both the deployed selector and the top-score rule.
- **Training the own decoder longer on teacher labels.** Its targets are SAM 3's own proposals, which miss the same objects.

## What might not work, and how we notice
- **Aliases and vetoed queries may add look-alikes**, above all on frames where the object is absent. The 456 both-good rows must not lose more than 1 point (bar `phrase_safe`), and the kit counts masks served on raster-absent frames (976 today).
- **K1 vocabulary may not be the benchmarks'.** The gain sits on ScanNet++ names; the R0-dev look reports by dataset.
- **239 captures may overfit the detector.** Fold-held-out pricing shows it; the fail-fast compares held-out objects only.
- **The tracker may drift where frames are far apart.** Anchors sit a median of 272 native frames apart on ScanNet++, which carries most of the gap, against 35 on ScanNet and 25 on ARKitScenes; the dense grid halves that. The raster-seeded run isolates the tracker, the kit reports by dataset, and member precision below 90% closes the object arms again.
- **Fixed objects may still be boxed badly.** Rows with both operands good score 75, the ceiling of every candidate without fusion; scale and pose errors remain.
- **My categories use thresholds (IoU 0.5, 0.1, precision 0.2) chosen once, before looking at scores.** sb_maskfail's interventions, built independently, give the same ordering.
