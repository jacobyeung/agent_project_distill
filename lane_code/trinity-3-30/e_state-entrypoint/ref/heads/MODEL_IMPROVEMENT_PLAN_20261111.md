## Question
The user (memory `lane-intent-two-goals-20261111.md`): "train new sota spatial understanding vlm - i strongly believe we can beat astra, argon, carbon with our 9B model! with GT perception we get 80+% on the benchamrks right? how can we improve our current model? how much data dowe need? do we need to add a different vision encoder? change the way attention works so we can get better pose and depth prediction? do we need to add a g3t or 4d reconstruction as a vision encoder? how to combine those features with the current vlm?" The user also said that the reasoning trace should use pose and depth to say where things are, that explicit chain-of-thought training should do better, that reinforcement learning can come on top, and that evaluation may use as many frames as helps.

The student is Qwen3.5-9B (LM and ViT) with a frozen G3T geometry tower (pose, depth), a grounding path over SAM 3 masks, and routed answers: the model writes a short program in its reasoning that calls its own heads inside `generate()` and copies the printed values into the answer. Training rows show that form with ground-truth values in the tool responses (head-backed traces). Compute: one 4 x H100 Orchard slot for full fine-tuning; frozen-LM stages and every read on Trinity 48 GB cards, after the main project.

## Answer
**The bottom line.** Geometry is no longer the wall, and grounding is. With the deployable stack of today (SAM 3 masks chosen by the selector, the frozen tower's pose, G3T's depth and a type-aware fusion rule in the runtime), scripted programs beat f17's direct answers on absolute distance (+9.2 [+3.8, +14.5]) and room size (+12.4 [+4.5, +21.2]), tie on relative distance and the three direction types, and lose on size (-5.8) and counting (-16.7) (M, 1,493 VSI-Bench ScanNet items, `gt_ceiling/events/20261111T1648Z_RESULT_t6_2_policy_selector_masks.md`). With ground-truth masks the same geometry scores 91.5 on relative distance, 98.1 on counting and 95 to 100 on direction, so masks bind every type except size and room. Those cells were priced on benchmark scenes; they diagnose which component binds, and every choice the leg freezes is made again on scenes outside the benchmarks (section C). The ledger supports 61.3 to 66.2 on VSI-Bench for the next leg at m200, against f17's 63.79 and a bar of about 77.

1. **"80+ with GT perception?"** Yes. On the ScanNet parts, taking each type's better path with ground-truth inputs gives VSI-Bench 90.1 against f17 direct's 64.3 and VSTI 97.8 against 78.3 (M, `GT_CEILING_DECOMPOSITION_20261111.md`, 2,071 + 5,736 items). This is a necessary condition, not a score.
2. **How do we improve the current model?** In this order: (a) put the fusion policy, G3T depth and the selector into the runtime's box computation and re-price them there; (b) train one checkpoint (LEG-S1) on v3.14 head-backed rows for the types where routing wins and on direct rows with a bounded thinking budget for the rest, with no program-form rows for route planning; (c) spend the next perception effort on masks, because on the mask-bound tasks the selector keeps about a tenth of what ground-truth masks give under the same geometry (section A).
3. **How much data?** The call form needs little: CP1 wrote camera calls at a 100% rate after 200 steps, about 640 routed rows per type (E from its mix), and the pool holds 35,126 admitted head-backed rows on ScanNet and ScanNet++ scenes and 11,819 on ARKitScenes (M, `dose_pool/events/20261111T1701Z_RESULT_T0_gaps_render.md`, `20261111T1703Z_RESULT_ARKit_rows.md`). The direct path is the data-hungry one: published models above 72 on VSI-Bench train on 0.6-8.5M rows, and no transferable rows-per-point law exists. The leg logs per-type rows consumed against scores (section F).
4. **A different vision encoder?** No swap for the LM. The Qwen ViT grounds well (RefCOCO 89.7) and carries no readable pose; geometry comes from the frozen tower. Whether another reconstruction model replaces G3T as the tower is decided by the bake-off (section D).
5. **Change attention for pose and depth?** No. The depth errors that broke fusion were per-frame scale noise in our own depth head (log-sd 0.077) and foreground-background mixing at mask boundaries; a fusion rule in the runtime recovers +18 to +40 points, and G3T's own depth already has a per-frame scale log-sd of 0.021 (M, `gt_ceiling/events/20261111T1632Z_RESULT_tower_geometry.md`, `20261111T1656Z_RESULT_t6_3_depth_scale_correction.md`). Tower pose alone costs 2 to 4 points per type.
6. **G3T or a 4D reconstruction model as an encoder?** G3T stays inside the model as a frozen tower and becomes the source of depth as well as pose. A 4D encoder waits for dynamic benchmarks and the VLA.
7. **How to combine the features?** Through two channels. Values reach the LM as text printed by tool calls, which carries the routed types. Features reach the LM through the GF-1 connector (a multi-layer G3T bank added to the visual tokens through a zero-initialised matrix), which serves the direct types and joins the leg after its GPU canary.
8. **Are pose and depth all you need?** No. Pose and depth say where a located pixel is; the answer also needs the right pixels of the whole object. On VSI-Bench the deployable stack gives a scenario of 65.3 to 66.5, perfect geometry with today's masks 69.4, and perfect masks with today's geometry 81.5 (E arithmetic on M cells, section A).
9. **Explicit CoT training and RL?** Yes to traces whose every number is printed by a tool call; the routed camera system beats direct answering by +59.2 on 200 official VSTI items. Program-form traces without a tool behind them hurt: CP1 learned a navigation step program for route planning that loops to the output cap or collapses to a first-turn guess (26.0 against f17's 58.0 on 50 items). RL comes after the leg, under the conditions in section G.
