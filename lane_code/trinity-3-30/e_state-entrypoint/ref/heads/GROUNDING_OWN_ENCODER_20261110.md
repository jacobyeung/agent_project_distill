# Grounding from the student's own vision pathway, with SAM 3 as the teacher

Design lane d_ownenc, 2026-11-10 11:02Z, trinity-2-18, read-only outside the lane. Nothing was trained, downloaded or launched. FACT = measured in this project, source named. SRC = primary web source [Wn], listed at the end. E = estimate. HYP = hypothesis that a named experiment settles. **dev** = 858 questions on 84 held-out ScanNet scans (589 scan/name objects): 31 tuning scans (221 objects) and 33 room-disjoint read scans (211 objects, 283 questions). Gains are routed-or-direct minus T0 m800 direct, in points, in the order direction medium / hard / relative distance / absolute distance. **On target** = the selected candidate overlaps the GT object. Branch **S** = SAM 3 as a frozen tower, **N** = the LM's native boxes with no training, **O** = the own pathway designed here.

## Question
What replaces SAM 3 at inference so that the 9B student resolves a named object from its own vision pathway, how do we choose that pathway cheaply, and how does SAM 3 teach it?

## Answer
1. **Build branch O as a grounding head on encoders the model already runs. Do not swap the LM's encoder.** Behind the unchanged `<ground obj=NAME>` call: a presence head ranks all 32-64 frames; the student's own ViT (same frozen weights) re-reads the four top frames at up to 1024 x 768 and hands its patch tokens to a decoder, never to the LM (0 extra LM tokens); a ~45M SAM 3-style decoder proposes instances with masks, boxes and an identity embedding; the object-first linker (built) forms at most five candidates; a small selector head picks one or abstains. G3T's trunk tokens, which the forward already computes on every frame, join as a second key stream for identity across frames. Lifting, propagation and trace text stay as in branch S.
2. **SAM 3 teaches shape and ranking; scan ground truth teaches identity.** SAM 3's native-gate masks are the mask targets, its scores are soft ranking targets, and its off-target instances are the hard negatives. The GT instance rasters say which instance each mask belongs to. A presence label trains only where GT and SAM 3 agree, because a blind gold reference overturns 11.4% of the masks' presence labels (FACT, label_ops 1047Z).
3. **Selection gates both branches, and the name underdetermines it.** SAM 3's own score puts the GT object first for 80.0% of dev objects (76.0% tuning, 83.9% clean), and its top five hold it for 94.1%. A frontier labeler choosing among those five reaches 82.3%; for 41% of objects it finds another candidate that also fits the name, and SAM 3's first choice is the GT object or such a fitting object for 94.9% (FACT, label_ops 1042Z; the last two figures are my count from its labels). So no verifier that sees only the name passes the tower, the student's included (FACT, X2). **Branch O's bar is parity with S at 50M added weights instead of 850M, not a selection win.** One lever remains on the name alone: the dataset's naming convention, which GT identity on training rooms can teach. Probe task T1 measures it on the tower's candidates, and a gain of 5 points ships in branch S.
4. **Choose the dense keys with probe P0** (frozen features, at most 4 GPU-h, rules fixed in section 2). Arms: the Qwen ViT at two resolutions, the depth head's ViT, G3T tokens, C-RADIOv4-SO400M, DINOv3 ViT-L, and SAM 3's encoder as the reference. My prior: the Qwen ViT suffices for name choice and presence and trails on masks and identity, because its SigLIP 2 family is documented weak there (ADE20k linear 44.0 against 54.9, instance retrieval 23.0 against 63.1, tracking 64.8 against 79.9 for DINOv3 ViT-L [W1]).
5. **If the own keys fail the mask rule, add C-RADIOv4-SO400M as a frozen dense tower** (431M, NVIDIA open model licence, distilled from SigLIP2-g, DINOv3-7B and SAM 3 [W3]). It ranks ahead of DINOv3 ViT-L (gated licence, no language alignment) and ahead of unfreezing the shared ViT (needs an H100 leg and puts the direct path at risk). A private fine-tuned copy of the Qwen ViT, as the depth head already has, is the route that later removes the extra tower.
6. **Do not adopt SAM 3's encoder as the LM's encoder.** It emits 5,184 patch tokens per frame, 1,296 after its own pooling, which is 41,472 tokens at 32 frames; aligning it needs an LM leg of several hundred H100-h (E).
7. **Cost.** Probe: at most 4 GPU-h. Teacher pass on the 8,000 local training frames: 1-2 GPU-h. Pilot: at most 4 A6000-h. Full head training with the LM frozen: 30-60 A6000-h (E; MDG-A took 40 with the LM in the loop). No H100 hour beyond the planned own-value leg.
8. **Paper.** One table with the pipeline fixed and the mask source varied (direct, N, MDG-A, O, S, S with the own selector, oracle), and a non-inferiority rule fixed in advance on two object-level shares: on target, and name-consistent (section 4).
