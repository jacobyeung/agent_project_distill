# Pose accuracy bridge: how accurate must our camera pose be for the head-backed programs? (2026-11-08)

**Every accuracy below is SENSITIVITY on benchmark items**: 655 ScanNet items of VSI-500 and VSTI-450, which sit inside the benchmarks we report. Do not set the routing policy from these numbers; set it on derivgen's held-out routing set (the 38 readout scenes). The reference pose errors of f17 (the 38-scene row), CPDM, PDG v3 and G3T were measured on those 38 held-out readout scenes (`/scratch/jjyeung/orch_cd/head_acc/REPORT.md` T1). f17's harness-scene errors and all MapAnything errors were measured here, on benchmark scenes.

## Question
1. The user said that 45 degrees of pose error is probably not enough to answer any question accurately. How accurate must our own camera pose be for each question type?
2. If our pose were as accurate as Cambrian-P's or G3T's, how well would the routed programs do? The user's hypothesis: G3T-level pose lets the programs answer at SPLIT accuracy (here, the same programs with GT tools: 80.1), which must beat the best fine-tuned models.
3. (Coordinator, final-run plan E3) What does each other error kind cost (depth, FoV, gravity, clicks), and where does each question family break even against direct answering?

## Answer
- **Most VSTI items need no pose.** Programs for camera-object distance, object-object left/right and near/far, object size and appearance order read one frame, so they score the same at 45 degrees and at 100% centre error (299 of 448 VSTI items and 65 of 207 VSI items). Up/down needs only gravity. Pose matters for counting, VSI direction, room size, relative distance, camera displacement (camera centres and scale only) and camera movement direction.
- **Yaw error costs, tilt error barely does.** At 15 degrees, yaw-only error drops VSI from 74.6 to 63.9, while the same angle about a random axis drops it to 68.8. Tilting the whole world by up to 10 degrees costs 0.4 points.
- **The user is right for the multi-frame VSI types.** At 45 degrees of yaw the VSI programs score 53.4 against 61.8 for f17's direct answers. Each type falls below direct at a yaw error of 7.6 deg for counting, 10 deg for medium direction, 15 deg for hard direction, 17.5 deg for room size, 20 deg for relative distance and 20 deg for VSI overall. Camera movement direction stays above direct even at 45 deg. Measured as a fraction of each camera's distance from frame 1, the centre error breaks even at 17% for counting, 39% for room size, 57% for camera displacement and 85% for VSI overall.
- **f17's pose erases the program advantage.** On these scenes f17's views are off by 14.7 deg (yaw 13.2 deg), and their centres by 0.49 of the distance from the reference view. With GT clicks and GT depth, f17's actual pose scores 69.5 on the 400 items that use only the frames its heads answer. GT pose scores 79.0 there and direct 70.0, and the matched simulation predicted 68.1. At f17's error level a 15% rotation gain is worth +0.1 VSI points [90% CI -0.8, +1.0], so the plan's question (is a 15% rotation gain worth at least 1 VSI point?) gets a no. A 15% centre gain is worth +1.0 [+0.5, +1.5].
- **G3T-level pose reaches SPLIT accuracy only for rotation.** G3T's 2.3 deg rotation costs nothing (80.1, the GT score). Its centre error costs the rest: with G3T's training-scene scale (0.24 of the distance) the programs score 75.5 (VSI 67.7, VSTI 79.0), and with a per-scene oracle scale (0.07) they score 78.3, against 80.1 with GT pose and 73.0 direct. The loss sits in camera displacement (96 to 68), counting (74.5 to 43) and room size (91 to 75). MapAnything confirms this with real poses. On these scenes its views are off by 4.0 deg and its centres by 0.29 of the distance, with a metric scale 24% too large. It scores 73.5 on its 335 items (GT 77.7, direct 70.2), and 75.3 after per-scene Sim(3) alignment.
- **With G3T-level pose, routing only the families where programs win clears direct by 6.7 to 9.6 points.** Under a rule fixed in advance (route camera motion and VSI geometry; keep easy direction, route, appearance and every single-frame VSTI type direct), the score is 79.7 with G3T's training-scene scale and 82.6 with its oracle scale, against 84.6 with GT pose and 73.0 direct. With f17's pose the same rule scores 72.4, below direct. Each of these keeps GT clicks and GT depth, so they are ceilings.
- **Other error kinds (VSI multi-frame geometry family, 162 items).** Depth error breaks even at 14% per point or 19% per frame (f17's depth AbsRel is 0.10 to 0.19), and wrong-instance clicks at 15%. FoV errors of ±10 deg, click jitter of 50 grid units and gravity tilt of 10 deg stay above direct. f17's predicted FoV costs nothing (+0.2). ScanNet++'s FoV on ScanNet frames costs 1.7 points overall and 5.3 on VSI, with 3 direction, 3 counting and 17 absolute-distance items worse; by the FoV record's rule (at least 3 flips) a per-dataset FoV gate is launch-blocking.

## Setup
- **Harness.** The toolprog programs (SPEC v3.6; `programs.py` scheme `spec`, variant `pts`) run unchanged from `/scratch/jjyeung/orch_cd/toolprog/work/tp/`. The harness loads 657 ScanNet items (VSI-500 ScanNet 207 + VSTI-450 450), and 655 of them have an f17 direct answer (207 + 448); those 655 are the item set. OWN_HEAD_RESULT's header ("201") is a typo. My driver reproduces `COND_GF-pts-spec_aws1` with no item mismatch.
- **One error at a time.** Clicks come from the GT instance raster, depth from the GT mesh raster and poses from ScanNet; each condition changes one input. Scoring uses MRA for numeric types and accuracy for choices; "direct" is f17's own answer on the same item.
- **E1, pose sweeps.** Frame 1 (the first valid frame) stays exact. Every other frame gets, independently, (a) a rotation of a fixed angle about its camera centre, with the axis uniform on the sphere or (b) about gravity only, at 2, 5, 10, 15, 25 and 45 deg; or (c) a centre shift of 5, 10, 25, 50 or 100% of its distance from frame 1 in a uniform direction. Three seeds, with the same random axes reused at every level.
- **E2, MapAnything.** `AD/dense_backbones/mapanything_dense_373_32_outputs` and `..._ransac_dense_outputs` cover 88 of the harness's 311 scenes, and their 32 frame indices equal the harness's in all 88. Their camera poses replace GT, either Sim(3)-aligned per scene on camera centres (upper bound) or anchored at frame 1 with MapAnything's own metric scale (no fit). I did not run the depth substitution (see IO).
- **E3, references.** Each reference's per-view errors are drawn log-normal with its median and IQR, uniform axis; f17-like and CPDM runs use yaw plus a 4.2 deg tilt. Centre errors are drawn the same way as a fraction of the distance from frame 1. Two real substitutions check the simulation: f17's heads (even frames) and MapAnything.
- **E4, other error kinds.** The kinds are depth per point (fixed relative error with a random sign, applied to each lifted point), depth scale per frame, FoV per frame (±deg, random sign), gravity tilt per scene, click jitter (a fixed offset on the 0-1000 grid) and wrong-instance clicks (each visible instance of the asked object swapped, with probability p, for a random visible instance of another object). E4 also covers the FoV record's K conditions (b)-(e), trajectory scale ±10/25% and the 15% rotation gain.
- **Families.** Types pool into families. Each family's program-minus-direct difference gets an item-paired bootstrap (4,000 resamples, 90% CI) on three-seed item means. Break-even is where the mean difference crosses 0.
