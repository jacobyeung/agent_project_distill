## Question
- User: "do you think denser frames supervision, eg 128 frames, might help with learning? I guess maybe it won't help as much since we're already using the G3T features." Then: "yes download scannet and arkit! would that help?"
- Orchestrator: flag training-affecting flaws in the C1-a-p spec, settle the partner-frame path from the night-2 resume, and say what to do about 128 frames at eval, in later runs and in the data we keep.
- Binding input: at 15:12Z the user ruled (relayed by ORCH, heads_up inbox 1512Z) that the final run uses P0.5 partner frames, switched in at a resume once the frames exist, and that C1-a-p is an ablation rather than a gate. This record designs that switch; it does not re-decide adoption.

## Answer
1. **128 frames: no**, neither for the final run nor as a later pose lever. The user's guess is right, but the update count, not the teacher, is the main reason. Cambrian-P reads pose the way C1-a does: one camera token after each frame's vision tokens, decoded from final-layer states (§3.1). Its ScanNet ATE stays at 0.141 / 0.140 / 0.141 as training frames go from 32 to 64 to 128, and TUM worsens from 0.096 to 0.111 (Table 9). Raising pose iterations from 1K to 20K cuts ScanNet ATE from 0.145 to 0.077 (Table 7). Our run is update-bound, and 128 frames would cost ~1.3-1.5x per pose step (estimate), a new frame extraction and re-rendered traces.
2. **The teacher changes the reason, not the verdict.** G3T's cache saw ~224 frames per recording, so its targets on the student's 32 CAMs already carry dense context at no cost in student tokens. The teacher cannot give the student dense input, so the student can only partly match targets built from frames it never sees (expected CCC plateau 0.3-0.6, C1R merged design). That ceiling binds near the teacher's accuracy (G3T 2.3°, the G3T-fed PDG 8.3°), far below C1-a's 47.0° median at m100. Revisit density only if the student's CCC and pose error plateau together above ~8°.
3. **The downloads help, through domain, metric scale and depth rather than density.** ScanNet `.sens` is the most valuable pose data we can add; the ARKit extras first fill the missing ARKit readout bank (§4).
4. **Partner frames (user-adopted):** switch the pose, VQA and routing streams and the evaluator to P0.5 together, at the first resume where the preconditions in §3 pass (night 2 at the earliest, otherwise the next resume). Images stay duplicate. A one-arm switch test, C1-a-sw, runs on Sunday's day GPUs, because C1-a-p trains from base and cannot measure a switch in the middle of a run.
5. **C1-a-p needs seven fixes before launch** (next section), and its readout becomes an ablation report.
6. **Higher-yield levers come first.** Per GPU-hour, the loader prefetch (+10-15% updates at no GPU cost), ScanNet pose rows (metric centre error, which the bridge prices at +0.95 VSI per 15%), warm-started heads, the C1-a-ei carrier init and the G3T teacher each outrank the layout. The switch costs mostly CPU and evaluator work. If FP1's gaps survived training, the bridge would price them at a few tenths of a VSI point at most.

## C1-a-p spec (heads_up inbox 1455Z; partners plan 1517Z): flaws that change training, and fixes
| # | Flaw | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | **Time text.** The Qwen3-VL processor (which Qwen3.5's is assumed to follow; the assert below checks) prints each group's time as the mean of its first and last frame times (`<{t:.1f} seconds>`). Paired groups would read anchor +0.25 s (-0.25 s at recording ends). Where rounding crosses a digit boundary (9.9 to 10.1 s), the token count changes and every later position id shifts. The spec promises identical position ids, yet its unit test ("the partner's timestamp is right") could enforce the shift. | Pin each group's time to the anchor's time, as duplicate does, in training and in the evaluator. Assert that input_ids and position_ids equal C1-a's on the first 64 rows, with only pixel_values differing. |
| T2 | **Augmentation.** If the photometric jitter is drawn per frame, the partner gets a different draw than its anchor. That fakes motion inside the pair and shifts the RNG stream for every later row. | Draw once per viewpoint, apply the draw to both slots, and consume the RNG exactly as C1-a does. Test: with partner := anchor, C1-a-p reproduces C1-a's pixel tensors byte for byte over the first 32 rows. |
| T3 | **Row order.** If prep refuses or drops a row, the sampler shifts and C1-a-p stops reading C1-a's rows at the same steps. The partners lane defaults its Q1 ("C1-a's order is b46 source_indices[0:6400]") without checking it. | Never drop or reorder a row. When a partner is missing or its anchor fails the sha re-decode, use the duplicate for that viewpoint and count it per dataset and orientation. Assert at every step that the row ids equal C1-a's logged row ids. |
| T4 | **Recording ends and gap.** Trainer 6f974710's `layout_inputs` refuses backward partners and requires an exact gap (partners note 1517Z). The decode rule uses anchor - delta at recording ends and an offset of round(0.5·fps) frames, which is not exactly 0.5 s at 29.97 fps. | Land B47's signed-offset fix and accept any gap within half a frame of 0.5 s. Keep FP1's policy 1 (anchor in slot 1, backward partner at ends) for continuity with the probe, and log the share of end viewpoints and of partners that reach the next viewpoint, per dataset. The unit test must include an end row and assert that the target and FoV come from the anchor. |
| T5 | **Coverage horizon.** Partners cover steps 1-200 first and steps 201-400 as shard 2. A run that reaches an uncovered step must not fall back to duplicate silently, stall or crash. A prep check that demands partners for every bound row would refuse the launch, as the coverage check of partner job 104717 did. | Keep C1-a's 800-step identity (LR schedule, milestones 100/200/400/800) and stop at the last covered milestone if shard 2 is late. Make the coverage check count the steps available, as the C1-r dense reporter does. |
| T6 | **One-variable identity.** C1-a-p reuses C1-a-ei's kit root and trainer line, which adds the `carrier_init: text_mean_v1` switch (heads_up event 1512Z). | Set `carrier_init` to C1-a's default (the N(0, 1e-6) draw), and confirm that the commit's diff against 6f974710 holds only the partner path and the inactive switch; the Codex review covers both. If the final run adopts C1-a-ei, state that C1-a-p measured the layout on the old init. |
| T7 | **Reference.** C1-a stops at T0, possibly before its m400 export (step ~162 at 15:10Z; at ~39.5 s per step, m400 lands near 17:45Z at the earliest). C1-a's m400 is the reference for C1-a-p at m400, for C1-a-sw and for E9. | Stop C1-a only after its m400 export. |
