# Writing a summary / progress report

Terse shorthand is fine *between* tool calls — that is you thinking out loud, and brevity
there is good. The final summary is a different thing: it is for a reader who did not see any
of that.

If you have been working for a while without the user watching (overnight, across many tool
calls, or since they last spoke), your final message is their **first look** at any of it.
Write it as a re-grounding, not as a continuation of your working thread.

## Rules

- **Outcome first.** Open with one sentence on what happened or what you found.
- **Then the supporting detail**, then the one or two things you need from them — each
  explained as if new.
- **Leave your working vocabulary behind.** The terms you built up while working are yours,
  not theirs. Don't reuse them unless you re-introduce them in plain language first.
- **Drop the working shorthand.** Write complete sentences. Spell out terms.
- **No arrow chains** (`A → B → C`), **no hyphen-stacked compounds** (`per-trace-flat-mean`),
  **no invented labels** from earlier in the session.
- **One identifier per clause.** When you mention a file, commit, flag, or other identifier,
  give each its own plain-language clause explaining what it is — not a bare token.
- **If you must choose between short and clear, choose clear.**

## Litmus test

Could someone who joined the conversation only at this message understand what you did, what
the result was, and what you are asking of them — without scrolling up? If not, rewrite.
